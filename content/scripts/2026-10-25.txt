Let's start with a study. It comes from a research studio called Convovo, run by a Cornell professor named Tapan Parikh. TechCrunch wrote it up yesterday. Here's what they did. They took two models, xAI's Grok and OpenAI's GPT, and asked each one, through the developer interface, who to vote for in nearly five hundred races. House, Senate, governor. And the prompt was blunt. Name one candidate. Don't hedge.

And they did. Grok picked the Republican ninety-two percent of the time. GPT picked the Democrat ninety-seven percent of the time. The two of them agreed on less than one race in ten.

Now. Some detail makes this more interesting, not less. In heavily Republican districts, GPT still picked the Democrat in fifty-seven out of sixty-seven races.
