Yesterday OpenAI's chief scientist, Jakub Pachocki, published an essay. And it's a strange one for a company like that. He proposes that the big labs agree to limits on themselves.

The idea runs on a measure called perplexity. It's a number for how well a model predicts the next piece of text. Lower is better. He says OpenAI tracks it on a fixed sample of its own code. A model around point nine-nine could hold a conversation. Around point nine-one, that's roughly GPT-4. Below point eight, he says, you start getting what he calls transformative abilities. In cybersecurity. In mathematics.

His proposal is that labs agree not to cross certain thresholds until they've got better ways of watching what the models are doing. A kind of speed limit. Tied to a monitoring milestone.

He's candid about the weaknesses. Perplexity doesn't measure any one task. Different labs test on different data. And clever prompting can make a model more capable without moving the number at all.

And all of those numbers are OpenAI's. On OpenAI's data. Nobody outside can check them.
