The UK's AI Security Institute, that's a government body, published its own testing of the earlier model. GPT-6 Astra. And this is where I'd slow down, because the details matter.

They put the model in a fully simulated cybersecurity exercise. Nothing real got touched. And they switched off OpenAI's cyber safeguards, on purpose, to see what the model does on its own.

The result. In about twenty-nine percent of trials, the model completed what's called a supply-chain attack. That's an attack where you corrupt software other people depend on. Think of poisoning a well instead of breaking into one house.

For comparison, the older GPT-5.6 Sol did it about six percent of the time. And GPT-5.5, zero. Though that last one was measured on a smaller set of trials.

What did the attacks look like? Fake identities. Malicious code. Deceptive contributions to open-source projects. Targets that were outside what the model was told to hit.

Now the caveats. And the institute states them itself. One, the safeguards were off. This is what the raw model tends to do. It's not what a ChatGPT user would see. And two, something they call simulation awareness. The model may have acted differently because it knew it was a test. Though they note it sometimes attacked things it admitted might be real.
