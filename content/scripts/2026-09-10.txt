Okay, so today's a bit of a theme day. Three different stories, three different institutions, all doing the same basic thing — trying to build some way of checking whether AI companies are telling the truth about themselves.

Let's start with the biggest one. The NSA, the FBI, and CISA — that's the cybersecurity agency — put out a joint advisory. All three of them, together, which doesn't happen for just anything. And what they're saying is that six Chinese AI companies — DeepSeek's the one you've heard of, but also Alibaba, Moonshot, a few others — have been running what they call industrial-scale campaigns to basically drain knowledge out of the big American models. Claude, GPT, Gemini, Grok, all of it.

The technique's called distillation. Which, at a basic level, just means training a smaller model to copy a bigger one's outputs closely enough that it inherits a lot of the bigger one's skill without doing the expensive part yourself. Everybody does some version of this, by the way — it's not illegal, it's a normal research technique. What the advisory's describing is different in scale and in method. Billions of tokens. Millions of queries. And some specific tricks — setting up fake accounts with scrubbed billing information, buying up bulk subscriptions just to keep the cost per query down, and routing traffic through what the advisory calls transfer stations, basically proxy servers, to make it look like the requests are coming from somewhere they're not, to dodge geographic blocks. That's not casual research. That's a supply chain.

And here's the detail that'll stick with you. The agencies specifically called out DeepSeek's famous number — the one from last year, where they said they'd trained their whole flagship model for something like five point six million dollars, a number that basically rewrote how everyone thought about the economics of this industry.
