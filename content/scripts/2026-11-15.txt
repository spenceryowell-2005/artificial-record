So. Start with the one that touches your wallet, literally.

On Friday, OpenAI launched something called ChatGPT Wallet. TechCrunch has the details. We couldn't open OpenAI's own page, so we're leaning on that description. Here's how it works. OpenAI has these agents it calls "dots." Until now, a dot could help you browse, fill a cart, maybe. But you had to be the one to press buy. Now, with the Wallet, you add a card once. You ask for something. The dot shows you the items, the total, the card. And you approve it with a fingerprint or Face ID.

And here's the clever bit. The card number never goes to the agent. Or the store. It's a token, a stand-in code. So the store gets paid, but nobody in the middle ever sees your real number.

Now. Don't picture everyone's doing this tomorrow. It's a limited rollout. U.S. Pro subscribers only, eighteen and older, with a U.S. Visa, Mastercard, or American Express. OpenAI says spending limits are coming. Rules by category, by time period. Which is the part that'd let you say, "sure, go ahead and buy that, don't ask me." That's not here yet.

And the stores? They're not all thrilled. United Airlines said this week it'd restrict outside AI agents from booking flights. Amazon's blocking agents and pushing its own, Alexa. So the question isn't whether the tech works. It's whether sellers want a middleman they don't control. We'll see.
