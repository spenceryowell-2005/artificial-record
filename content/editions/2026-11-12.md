## The Executive Read

The company that sells the hardware under most of the AI industry lost its founder on Thursday. Nvidia said in a press release and an SEC filing that Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive and director, effective immediately. The release gives no reason. Colette Kress, the chief financial officer, becomes interim CEO and keeps her finance job, and Harvey C. Jones becomes the company's first board chair. The resignation follows days of reporting, and a statement from Huang on Sunday, about a relationship with a woman linked to Megaspeed, a Singapore-based Nvidia customer that U.S. officials are reported to suspect worked for Chinese intelligence. China calls that claim a smear, and Nvidia's board says it has no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised. The board's outside investigation, run by the law firm Wachtell Lipton, is still open. The throughline for the day is concentration. One company, and until today one person, sits at the centre of the chip supply, the government's export-control worries and a web of financing deals, and the rest of the industry is now reading Nvidia's governance as a risk factor. Nvidia says it will still report quarterly results on November 17. Elsewhere, the UK government said it helped back a rival chip designer, Fractile, in a round the government's release puts at more than £500 million, and a federal judge in New York is weighing whether training AI on copyrighted books is fair use. Nothing here is investment advice.

## Top AI Headlines

### Nvidia Says Jensen Huang Has Resigned; CFO Colette Kress Is Interim CEO

**What happened.** Nvidia announced on November 12 that Huang resigned as president, chief executive and board member, effective immediately. Kress, who joined Nvidia in 2013, becomes interim CEO and stays CFO until a permanent successor is named. The board says it will consider internal and external candidates. Jones, a former chief executive and executive chairman of Synopsys, becomes Nvidia's first-ever board chair. Jones said in the release that Kress "has helped lead NVIDIA through a period of extraordinary growth," and the board said it expects operations, customer commitments and the product roadmap to continue "without interruption." Nvidia filed a Form 8-K, the report public companies must file for events like an executive departure, the same day. The release does not say why Huang left.

The background comes from the press. According to CNBC, Huang apologized on Sunday for being "unfaithful to my wife," without detail, in connection with Alice Huang, a businesswoman linked to Megaspeed. She is not related to him. CNBC also reports that The New York Times separately reported a second relationship, with a senior Nvidia executive. Yahoo Finance reports that U.S. intelligence officials believe Alice Huang is a Chinese spy. We have not seen those officials or any evidence, Reuters is reported to have been unable to verify the intelligence claim, and China has denied it. Huang has denied compromising national security or Nvidia's secrets. On Tuesday the board's independent members said they had hired Joon H. Kim, a former acting U.S. attorney in Manhattan, and his firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to investigate.

**Why it matters.** Fortune reported on November 9 that Nvidia is worth about $4.3 trillion, that Huang had been CEO for 33 years, held 3.56% of the shares with no special voting stock, and had about 60 direct reports. It also reported that Nvidia has no chief operating officer and has not publicly named a successor. Huang had said he hoped the next CEO already works there. CNBC reports Nvidia shares closed at $173.73 on Wednesday, down from a closing high of $239.24 on October 6, and heading for a sixth straight weekly decline, the longest slide since 2012. Yahoo Finance reported a premarket drop of more than 12% on the news; the quote moved through the morning, so treat that figure as a snapshot.

**Business implication.** Customers' contracts and the chip roadmap are, per the board, unchanged, and Kress has been Nvidia's finance chief since 2013. But the buyers of Nvidia hardware, from cloud providers to the AI labs, now have a vendor in a leadership search during an open investigation and a congressional request for a briefing. Procurement teams should ask their Nvidia account teams in writing what the transition changes for delivery schedules and pricing. Results on November 17 will be the first look at how Kress frames it.

The reasons for the departure, the New York Times report and the intelligence claim are press reporting, not Nvidia's statements.*

### The Megaspeed Case: Commerce Reportedly Paused Its Probe After Nvidia's Lawyer Reached Out

**What happened.** The New York Times reported on November 9, in an article by Ana Swanson and Devlin Barrett, that the Commerce Department stopped its investigation into Megaspeed after Nvidia's general counsel contacted the agents running it. Commerce's export-enforcement unit had suspected Megaspeed of smuggling Nvidia AI chips into China in breach of U.S. export rules. The Times reports the general counsel emailed the agents in August 2025, saying a senior Commerce official had approved the outreach; the agents declined a meeting and passed the emails up the chain, and some officials saw the contact as a possible procedural breach that could taint the case. The Justice Department then asked Commerce to pause, and the case moved to Justice, which is reportedly running a criminal inquiry. We could not open the Times' own page; we relied on The Next Web's summary of it, and the reporting is the Times'.

Commerce says it handled the case strictly by the book. Nvidia says its contacts were meant to answer questions and share information from the field. Megaspeed denies smuggling chips and says it is investigating itself. Nobody has been charged in anything we found.

**Why it matters.** This is the other half of Huang's trouble. The relationship story is personal. The export-control question is institutional: whether the world's dominant chip supplier knew its hardware was being diverted to China, and whether it leaned on regulators. Senator Elizabeth Warren, the top Democrat on the Senate Banking Committee, wrote to Nvidia on June 1 asking about its export-control compliance and the accuracy of its public statements on diversion. Nvidia says its statements were accurate. CNBC reports Democrats are poised to take both houses of Congress and have promised to investigate Nvidia. It also reports U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said the United States wants "crown jewel" chips kept in American or allied hands and that the AI build-out will continue.

**Business implication.** Companies that resell or host Nvidia hardware abroad, particularly in Southeast Asia, should expect more questions about where their chips physically sit and who has access. Keep deployment records current. A House briefing with Huang was requested for November 18; we have seen no reply, and his resignation may change who is asked.

*Sources: [The Next Web, summarizing the New York Times report (November 9)](https://thenextweb.com/news/megaspeed-probe-commerce-nvidia-lawyer-justice-department-nyt); [Senate Banking Committee minority staff, Warren letter to Nvidia (June 1)](https://www.banking.senate.gov/newsroom/minority/warren-probes-nvidias-compliance-with-export-control-laws-and-regulations); [CNBC live updates (November 12)](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/12/nvidia-jensen-huang-ceo-live-updates.html). The Times' reporting is unconfirmed by us; Commerce, Nvidia and Megaspeed dispute parts of it.*

### UK Government Says It Backed Fractile's Series C of More Than £500 Million, at Over £5 Billion

**What happened.** The UK government announced on November 11 that its Sovereign AI Venture Fund and the British Business Bank, a state-owned development lender, are investing in Fractile, a chip designer based in Bristol and London. The release says the Series C, a late-stage venture round, is worth over £500 million and values the company at more than £5 billion. Other investors named are CapitalG, Lightspeed and Redpoint. The government did not say how much it put in, calling the figure commercially sensitive, and noted that Sovereign AI's typical equity investments run about £1 million to £10 million. Fractile says it is building faster, cheaper and more energy-efficient AI hardware, and plans to expand in Bristol.

**Why it matters.** Fractile is trying to build chips for running trained models, which is where most AI spending is heading, in competition with Nvidia. The release is also a policy statement: the government says backing UK chip designers reduces reliance on overseas supply. The timing is pointed, on the same day Nvidia's leadership changed. We want to be careful here. Press reports before the announcement, including from the Telegraph and in company filings, put the round nearer $700 million or more, and valuations in some accounts were higher. Those are secondary reports and we have not reconciled them with the government's wording, so we use the government's figures.

**Business implication.** Cloud and enterprise buyers worried about single-vendor dependence now have another well-funded alternative on paper, but the government release says nothing about shipping products or customers. One report, which we could not confirm from a primary source, says deployments are planned for 2027. Treat it as a supply option to watch, not one to plan around.

*Sources: [UK Government, "Sovereign AI and British Business Bank invest in British firm developing the next generation of AI hardware" (November 11)](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/sovereign-ai-and-british-business-bank-invest-in-british-firm-developing-the-next-generation-of-ai-hardware). Figures are the government's; Fractile's own statement was not separately reviewed.*

### The OpenAI Copyright Case Reaches the End of Briefing, With More Than 50 Friend-of-the-Court Filings

**What happened.** In the consolidated case *In re OpenAI Copyright Infringement Litigation* in Manhattan, before Judge Sidney Stein, the last round of summary-judgment briefs was due November 6. Summary judgment is a request for the judge to decide a legal question without a trial, here whether training on copyrighted works is fair use. Publishing Perspectives reports the court received 51 amicus briefs, filings from outside groups. It counts 26 supporting the plaintiffs, 18 the defendants and eight neutral, though those figures add to 52, so its totals may be off by one. The plaintiffs include the Authors Guild and The New York Times. The Justice Department filed a statement of interest on September 4 urging the court to find AI training to be fair use, according to the same report. Oral argument is to be scheduled after November 6.

**Why it matters.** This is the largest pending test of whether AI developers need licences to train on books and news. The Publishing Perspectives account lists publishers' associations, PEN America and members of Congress on one side, and the Electronic Frontier Foundation, Public Knowledge and several law professors on the other. A ruling for either side would reset licensing negotiations across the industry. The same report says Judge Stein struck a study on AI books displacing human-written works from the evidence in October.

**Business implication.** There is no ruling yet and no hearing date we could verify. Companies that buy AI products trained on scraped data should check whether vendors offer indemnity, a promise to cover legal costs if a customer is sued over outputs, and under what conditions.

*Sources: [Publishing Perspectives (November 2026)](https://publishingperspectives.com/2026/11/copyright-infringement-lawsuit-against-openai-draws-more-than-50-very-illuminating-amicus-briefs/). We could not open the court docket (CourtListener returned an error), so dates and counts are the trade publication's.*

## Model and Product Updates

**Thinking Machines releases Inkling 2 Small, with open weights promised.** The company's post of November 5 describes a mixture-of-experts model, one that activates only part of its parameters for each request, with 594 billion total and 17 billion active parameters, and an image input encoder. Weights, the model's trained numbers, are currently closed. The company says it intends to release them under the permissive Apache 2.0 licence "next week," pending outside safety testing and red-teaming, which is a plan and not a release. The model is available now on its Tinker developer service. The benchmark figures are the company's own: 86.3% on SWE-bench Pro v2 (Hard) and a score of about 31 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, an outside index, though the post's text and table differ slightly on that number. The post says the model trails some closed models, including one it calls GPT-6.1 Sol, on its alignment evaluations. *[Thinking Machines, "Introducing Inkling 2 Small" (November 5)](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-small/)*

**Nvidia contributes OpenShell, an agent-security runtime, to the Cloud Native Computing Foundation.** Nvidia's November 10 post says it is donating OpenShell, software for securing and governing autonomous AI agents, to the foundation as a Sandbox project, the entry tier for open-source projects. It also says it is contributing to Agent Substrate, a Google-led framework for running long-lived agents on Kubernetes, the standard system for managing server clusters, and that the two projects are being integrated. It reports that hundreds of organizations use OpenShell. The post does not state licence terms. The announcement matters more after this week: the people running agents in production are asking who controls what an agent can reach. *[NVIDIA blog (November 10)](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/open-source-openshell-infrastructure/)*

**Anthropic has no new announcement today.** Its news page lists the November 10 roadshow post as the latest, ahead of the November 9 release of Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2, which we covered earlier this week. *[Anthropic newsroom](https://www.anthropic.com/news)*

## Regulation and Policy Watch

**U.S. preemption fight remains open.** A bill that would stop states from regulating AI has not passed. A tracker we checked says that as of November 4, no federal statute sets general rules for private AI systems or broadly overrides state AI laws. The White House asked Congress in March for a national framework that would override state laws, and a bipartisan discussion draft from Representatives Jay Obernolte and Lori Trahan in June would override state laws on AI development for three years. Neither has cleared Congress. A law-firm analysis argues that Democratic control of Congress makes such preemption less likely. That is a scenario, not an outcome, and we did not retrieve a primary source for the election results or the draft. We mention it because CNBC reports Democrats are poised to take both chambers.

**EU AI Act timing.** Reports from earlier this year describe a provisional agreement in May to delay the Act's high-risk rules to December 2027 for stand-alone systems and August 2028 for AI embedded in regulated products. We could not confirm formal adoption or publication in the Official Journal from sources we could open, so we do not state the final dates as settled.

**Anthropic IPO terms unchanged.** Anthropic's roadshow page, which we re-read today, still gives only the expected range of $70 to $78 a share and the proposed Nasdaq symbol "ANT." It states no pricing date, listing date or valuation, and does not mention any concurrent private sale. We did not find a source for a reported $26 billion placement and are not repeating it. *[Anthropic, roadshow announcement (November 10)](https://www.anthropic.com/news/roadshow-launch)*

## Forward-Looking Angle

The useful question is not who replaces Huang. It is how much of what looked like Nvidia's strength was one person's relationships. Huang had direct ties to the White House, to the labs that buy his chips, and to the partners he finances. Kress knows the numbers, and the board has said it has confidence in her. A permanent hire from inside or outside will tell customers whether the company intends to stay a relationship business or become a more conventional one. That is our reading, not a statement from Nvidia.
