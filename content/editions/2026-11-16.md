## The Executive Read

The theme today is verification, on the eve of the biggest test of the AI chip trade. Nvidia reports results Tuesday afternoon, and the week's other stories each ask whether a claim holds up. Google says its new Gemini 4 Hydrogen model beats its own older flagship on one index and is now in Search for all users; those numbers are Google's. A researcher at a nonprofit says the published weights of several Chinese models carry the signature of Nvidia's Blackwell chips, which are banned from export to China. His method has stated limits, and he does not claim to show a law was broken. Nebius, a cloud company that rents out Nvidia chips, reported quarterly revenue of $981 million, according to TechCrunch. TechCrunch also reports it cut its full-year forecast to $3 billion over delays at a New Jersey site that serves a Microsoft contract worth up to $19.4 billion. Anthropic is on its initial public offering roadshow with a range of $70 to $78 a share, and the company has not given a pricing date. Nothing here is investment advice. The common thread is that the buildout has to be delivered on time and on legal footing, and the evidence for both is still thinner than the headlines suggest.

## Top AI Headlines

### Google Puts Gemini 4 Hydrogen Into Search for All Users and Says Carbon's Price Will Double

**What happened.** Google announced Gemini 4 Hydrogen on Nov. 12 and said it is rolling out globally. It is a fast, lower-cost model for coding and "agentic" work, meaning tasks where the software takes several steps on its own. Google's own post says it is available in the Gemini API, Google AI Studio, the Gemini app and Google Search for all users. Priced at $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens, it has a one-million-token context window. A token is a small chunk of text, roughly three-quarters of a word. Separately, Google's Nov. 10 post on Gemini 4 Carbon, its strongest model, says the introductory price of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens ends Nov. 30. After that it rises to $4 and $20.

**Why it matters.** Every performance figure below is Google's own claim, not an independent result. Google says Hydrogen scores 51 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at its default setting, against 50 for its larger Gemini 4 Argon, and 54 at a new "xhigh" setting. It says Hydrogen finishes tasks about 2.5 times faster than Gemini 3.8 Flash and reduces fabricated claims by more than five times. For Carbon, Google reports first place on several partner benchmarks, including Cursor's, and cites partners' tests. Its post notes that several results come from internal or pre-release evaluations. Google's Search post calls the change its "biggest model upgrade to Search yet," which is a self-assessment. TechCrunch reports the same upgrade improves how AI Mode breaks complicated questions into sub-searches.

**Business implication.** The Carbon price change is the concrete item. Teams running Carbon should budget for double the per-token rate from Nov. 30, or test whether Hydrogen or Argon is enough for their work. Google's own post says Argon "remains the ideal choice for the majority of software engineering and knowledge work," which is a polite hint about where it expects most spend to go.

*Sources: [Google, "Introducing Gemini 4 Hydrogen" (Nov. 12)](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/); [Google, "Gemini 4 Carbon is now available" (Nov. 10)](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-carbon-now-available/); [Google, Search upgrade (Nov. 12)](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/gemini-4-hydrogen-search-upgrade/); [TechCrunch (Nov. 12)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/googles-latest-search-upgrade-promises-better-answers-to-your-most-complicated-questions/). All benchmark figures are Google's own.*

### A Researcher Says Top Chinese Open Models Carry Fingerprints of Export-Banned Blackwell Chips

**What happened.** Will DePue, a former OpenAI researcher who leads the nonprofit Unemployment Labs, published a report on Nov. 12 and revised it the same day. It says the published weights of six models from four Chinese labs match the layout of Nvidia's Blackwell-class chips. The models are DeepSeek-V4-Flash and V4-Pro, Kimi K3 and K3.1-Pro, MiniMax-M3 and Ant Group's Ling-3.1-flash. Weights are the numbers a model learns; they start from random values, and the report says how those starting values are generated depends on the number of processing units in the GPU used. The author says earlier models from the same labs match older Hopper-generation layouts. He says newer models from Alibaba, Zhipu, Tencent, Xiaomi and StepFun read as Hopper, and that Meituan's LongCat-2.0 showed no Nvidia fingerprint. TechCrunch covered the report on Nov. 13.

**Why it matters.** The report is careful about what it does not show. It says the fingerprint reveals the hardware that generated the starting weights, not necessarily the hardware that did the training. It says its findings do not establish where chips were located, how they were obtained, or whether export controls were violated. TechCrunch adds that the chips could have been in offshore data centers. The author's scale estimate is about 12,000 Blackwell GPUs across the five training runs, with a wide range of 7,000 to 22,000, resting on assumptions he calls uncertain. The method is the author's own and, as far as we saw, has not been independently replicated. He says it matched all 24 publicly disclosed hardware cases.

**Business implication.** The U.S. bans Blackwell sales to China. TechCrunch reports the administration allows the weaker H200 if shipments take a route that lets the U.S. collect a 25% tariff. If weight forensics holds up, it gives export-control officials a new, public tool for checking claims. It could also give Congress more material for its Nvidia inquiries. For now it is a researcher's claim, and Nvidia and the Chinese labs have not responded in anything we read.

*Sources: [Unemployment Labs, "Top Chinese Frontier AI Models Carry the Fingerprint of Export-Controlled NVIDIA Blackwell GPUs" (Nov. 12)](https://unemploymentlabs.com/checkpoint-forensics); [TechCrunch (Nov. 13)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/13/export-banned-nvidia-chips-are-behind-chinas-most-powerful-ai-models-uh-oh-jensen/).*

### Nebius Cuts Its 2026 Forecast and Plans About $40 Billion in 2027 Spending

**What happened.** Nebius, a Netherlands-based "neocloud," meaning a company that rents out AI computing power, released third-quarter results on Nov. 12. Its newsroom lists the release and a shareholder letter from founder Arkady Volozh; the PDFs holding the figures did not load for us, so the numbers below are TechCrunch's reading of Nebius's materials. Quarterly revenue was $981 million, up 571% from a year earlier, though about a third was a one-time payment tied to a new partnership model. TechCrunch says Nebius cut its 2026 revenue forecast by $200 million to $3 billion, blaming delays at third-party sites, and forecast $12 billion to $13 billion for 2027. It plans roughly $40 billion in spending next year, which is above Wall Street expectations per TechCrunch, and says customer prepayments and loans backed by customer contracts could cover up to 85% of it. Shares fell 17% on the day, per TechCrunch.

**Why it matters.** The delays concern a Vineland, New Jersey site run by a third-party operator, DataOne, that serves a Microsoft contract worth up to $19.4 billion. TechCrunch says just over half the capacity is planned for the end of 2026, with the rest in the second quarter of 2027. It also reports that, per Nebius's own summary of the contract in an SEC filing, Microsoft can end affected service after a grace period if dates are missed and no substitute capacity is offered, and the contract carries late-delivery penalties. Microsoft has not announced any cancellation, and Nebius did not say whether Microsoft agreed to the new dates. TechCrunch also reports that New Jersey regulators fined the site $1.1 million for gas generators run without permits, and that a noise lawsuit from neighbors is pending. We did not read the filing or the regulator's order ourselves.

**Business implication.** Chip demand is not the bottleneck here. Power, permits and construction are. For Tuesday's Nvidia report, that is the question behind the question: whether the customers buying its chips can switch them on. A Nebius pullback would not by itself mean weaker Nvidia orders, because Nebius says it has contracts and prepayments behind its plans. That claim is the company's own.

*Sources: [Nebius, "Nebius reports third quarter 2026 financial results" (Nov. 12)](https://nebius.com/newsroom/nebius-reports-third-quarter-2026-financial-results); [TechCrunch (Nov. 12)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/nebius-tests-the-neocloud-playbook-with-40-billion-in-planned-spending/), which links Nebius's filings. We did not read the PDFs ourselves.*

### Anthropic's Roadshow Is Under Way; Pricing Has No Confirmed Date

**What happened.** Anthropic launched the roadshow for its proposed IPO on Nov. 10, and its announcement says 825 million Class A shares are offered at an expected $70 to $78 each. Of those, 801,351,354 come from Anthropic and 23,648,646 from existing stockholders, who keep the proceeds. Underwriters hold a 30-day option on up to 123,750,000 more shares. The shares are proposed for Nasdaq under "ANT," with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley handling prospectus requests. The registration statement is not yet effective, so the shares cannot be sold. Anthropic filed its S-1, the registration document, on Oct. 26. The announcement gives no pricing or first-trading date.

**Why it matters.** Our Nov. 15 edition cited press reports pointing to pricing on Wednesday, Nov. 18. We could not find that date in any Anthropic document, and one search result suggested a later window; that is speculation, not company guidance. Treat any date as unconfirmed until the company or a final prospectus gives one. Valuation figures vary widely among outlets, so we do not quote one. The range itself, at the stated share counts, implies the company would raise roughly $56 billion to $62 billion before the option. That is our arithmetic from the announced numbers, not a company figure.

**Business implication.** A large listing of an AI lab would give the market a public price for a company that sells model access. Customers and rivals would also gain a regular stream of audited disclosures. Until the offering prices, there is nothing to trade, and a registration that is not yet effective can still change.

*Sources: [Anthropic, "Anthropic launches roadshow for proposed initial public offering" (Nov. 10)](https://anthropic.com/news/roadshow-launch); [Anthropic newsroom](https://www.anthropic.com/news), listing the Oct. 26 S-1 announcement.*

## Model and Product Updates

**Inkling 2 Small license resolved.** Our Nov. 15 edition said we could not confirm the license for Thinking Machines Lab's Inkling 2 Small. The Hugging Face model page now shows "License: apache-2.0," a permissive license. The page lists 594 billion parameters and image-and-text input. The listing displays "Updated 1 day ago" with no absolute date. *Source: [Hugging Face, thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Small](https://huggingface.co/thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Small).*

**Google DeepMind's Gemini Robotics 2.5.** DeepMind's Nov. 10 post calls it its most capable vision-language-action model, meaning one that turns camera images and instructions into robot movement. It says the model was tested on more than 200 tasks and reports 30% to 100% higher success than earlier baselines and tasks finished 20% to 30% faster. It reports 96% success for Apptronik's Apollo 2 humanoid on automotive parts kitting on Mercedes-Benz production lines in Germany and Hungary, described as a proof of concept. All figures are DeepMind's own. It is rolling out to robotics partners only. *Source: [Google DeepMind (Nov. 10)](https://deepmind.google/blog/gemini-robotics-2-5-accelerates-real-world-physical-intelligence/).*

**AlphaProof Code verifies Signal's cryptography.** Also Nov. 10, DeepMind said AI agents powered by Gemini Carbon produced over six million lines of Lean, a proof-checking language, to formally verify the compiled code of the Signal messaging protocol. Formal verification means a mathematical proof that code behaves as specified. DeepMind says the work took four weeks, that about 1% of the codebase was flagged for human review, and that it is the first such verification of a widely deployed cryptographic protocol at this depth. The claims are DeepMind's. The artifacts are open-sourced and a technical paper is linked. It is a research result, not a product. *Source: [Google DeepMind (Nov. 10)](https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-alphaproof-code/).*

**Meta's Muse can phone businesses, for paid users.** TechCrunch reports that Alexandr Wang, Meta's chief AI officer, announced on X that Muse can place calls to U.S. businesses for paid subscribers. Plans start at $20 a month for 500 million Muse tokens a week, with $100 a month for 3 billion. Meta says Muse has more than 10 million users. TechCrunch notes that many businesses hang up once they realize the caller is not human. *Source: [TechCrunch (Nov. 12)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/muse-can-now-place-calls-to-u-s-businesses-if-you-pay/).*

**Apple launches a study app on AI and health.** Apple's Apple Health AI Study app went live in the U.S. on Nov. 15, according to TechCrunch. It is built on technology from OpenEvidence, and users ask health questions and get answers grounded in clinical sources. Participants can optionally share Apple Health data. TechCrunch says Apple targets up to 200,000 participants over more than 12 months, that data is anonymized, and that Apple says it will not be used to train AI models. The App Store listing confirms it is free, needs iOS 26, and states that neither the app nor the study provides medical advice, diagnosis or treatment. The eligibility details, such as age 21 and an iPhone 15 or later, are TechCrunch's, not from the listing. *Sources: [Apple App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/apple-health-ai-study-app/id6800060365); [TechCrunch (Nov. 15)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/15/apple-launches-new-app-to-study-how-people-use-ai-for-health/).*

## Regulation and Policy Watch

**United Airlines restricts third-party AI agents.** United's Nov. 11 release says it will limit access to its digital platforms, and through travel agencies and aggregators, to block unauthorized personal AI agents. It says giving login details to outside assistants can expose sensitive information and cause unintended purchases or use of MileagePlus miles. It also argues that automated actions could conflict with FAA and Department of Transportation rules, noting that check-in requires a human to confirm bags hold no banned substances. That legal reading is United's, and we did not check it against the rules. It is company policy, not regulation, but it lands one week after OpenAI's Wallet launch, which lets agents complete purchases. United urged banking and retail companies to adopt similar safeguards. *Sources: [United Airlines newsroom (Nov. 11)](https://united.mediaroom.com/news-releases?item=125496); [TechCrunch (Nov. 12)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/united-airlines-blocks-ai-agents-from-booking-flights-citing-cybersecurity-risks/).*

**Export policy remains the open front.** We found no new federal rule on AI chips dated this week. The live items are the congressional inquiries covered in our Nov. 15 edition and the Blackwell-fingerprint report above. Two numbers from TechCrunch's Nov. 13 article, attributed there to Epoch AI and Reuters, give the scale of the enforcement problem. Epoch estimates about 2 million advanced chips entered China in 2024 and 2025 through schemes that hid their destination. Reuters reports about 600,000 H200 chips stockpiled in Hong Kong awaiting Chinese import approval. We did not read either primary source. *Source: [TechCrunch (Nov. 13)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/13/export-banned-nvidia-chips-are-behind-chinas-most-powerful-ai-models-uh-oh-jensen/).*

## Emerging Startup Radar

**Dreamteam raises $53 million for an AI-native CRM.** TechCrunch reports the San Francisco startup, founded in 2025 by former Freshworks executives, raised $53 million across seed and Series A rounds. Together Fund led the seed and Accel led the Series A, with Premji Invest participating. A CRM is the software sales teams use to track customers. Dreamteam says four built-in agents research prospects, draft follow-ups and flag deal risks, with salespeople approving what reaches customers. The company claims more than 100 customers in 13 countries, most paying, within three months of active sales. It did not disclose revenue or valuation, and a16z, which TechCrunch cites, argues incumbents will probably stay the system of record. *Source: [TechCrunch (Nov. 16)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/16/freshworks-alums-raise-53m-to-take-on-salesforce-hubspot-with-ai-native-crm/); we did not find a company announcement to cite.*

**Bharat Semi raises a $24 million seed.** The India- and U.S.-based startup makes programmable image sensors that combine capture, processing and AI on one chip. TechCrunch reports Info Edge Group, South Park Commons and Indus Bridge Ventures led. The company claims system costs could fall by at least 70% against separate parts, which is its own claim. It is pre-revenue, with first commercial production targeted for 2027, and it says General Atomics and Kaynes Semicon are design wins. It plans to stay fabless, and a separate Indian fab plan is not funded by this round. *Source: [TechCrunch (Nov. 15)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/15/indian-chip-startup-bharat-semi-lands-24m-seed-round-as-it-eyes-global-ai-sensor-market/).*

## AI Infrastructure and Market Signals

**Nvidia's reach, in figures.** TechCrunch's Nov. 15 analysis says Nvidia holds roughly 80% of the AI GPU market and cites reported figures of $99 billion in investments, $25 billion in commitments and $36 billion in cloud service commitments, many involving CoreWeave, Nebius, OpenAI and Anthropic. These are the outlet's figures; we did not trace them to a filing. TechCrunch's view is that even a small pullback by Nvidia could have wide effects because of this circular financing, in which Nvidia funds customers who then buy its chips. That is the outlet's analysis. Interim leadership, per the same piece, is CFO Colette Kress, described as a caretaker appointment with no succession plan in place. *Source: [TechCrunch (Nov. 15)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/15/is-bad-news-for-jensen-huang-also-bad-news-for-the-ai-industry/).*

**Delivery risk at the neoclouds.** The Nebius delays above are the clearest sign this week that sites, not chips, limit how fast capacity arrives. Nebius itself says prepayments and contract-backed loans could cover up to 85% of its 2027 spending, according to TechCrunch.

## Forward-Looking Angle

Three tests arrive close together. Tuesday's Nvidia report will show how the company describes customers' ability to turn chips on, a point Nebius made concrete. The Nov. 30 Carbon price change will show whether developers pay double or move to cheaper Google models. And the Blackwell-fingerprint method will show whether others can replicate it. Our reading, not a finding: public, checkable evidence is becoming the contested ground in AI, whether it is a benchmark, a weight file or a delivery schedule.
