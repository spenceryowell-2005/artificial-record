## The Executive Read

Saturday's news was about who holds the money and the keys. OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet, which lets its agents complete a purchase, but only for a limited set of U.S. Pro subscribers and only after the user approves it with a fingerprint or face scan. Thinking Machines Lab, which launched Inkling 2 Small on Nov. 5 with closed weights, now says the weights are public, so anyone can run the model. LangChain, a company that builds tools for managing AI agents, announced $300 million at a $3 billion valuation. Behind all three sits Nvidia, the company that supplies most of the chips and a large share of the startup capital. Its board accepted its chief executive's resignation on Thursday. Venture investors told TechCrunch that his exit leaves a gap in how young AI companies are funded, and the Wall Street Journal, as relayed by TechCrunch, reported that the interim chief had already scaled back Nvidia's commitment to an OpenAI data center. TechCrunch's account includes no Nvidia response to that report. Democrats in Congress have asked the Commerce Department to preserve records of the chip-sales arrangement with China; they cannot compel an answer. Nvidia reports quarterly results Tuesday afternoon, and Anthropic's expected pricing is Wednesday, according to press reports; the company has not confirmed the date. The throughline is trust in the intermediaries: who is allowed to spend on a user's behalf, who may fund the next lab, and who answers for chips that end up where they should not. Nothing here is investment advice.

### Nvidia's Board Faces a 100,000-Signature Petition and Questions About Its Startup Funding

**What happened.** According to TechCrunch, a petition at standwithjensen.com asking Nvidia's board to reinstate Jensen Huang had passed 115,000 signatures by Saturday afternoon. The site says its signers include 5,965 current Nvidia employees and 63,063 shareholders; we cannot verify those counts. Supporters named by TechCrunch include Elon Musk, Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney and Y Combinator CEO Garry Tan. The petition says Huang "deserves due process, not mob justice" and asks for an independent investigation. TechCrunch reports no Nvidia response and says the board has not changed course. Huang resigned Thursday after Nvidia's board opened an independent investigation on Nov. 8, following his admission of a relationship with a former executive of Megaspeed, a Singapore company. U.S. officials reportedly suspect that woman of links to Chinese intelligence. China's foreign ministry has called the espionage allegations "groundless." Those allegations remain unproven.

Separately, TechCrunch summarized a Wall Street Journal report, which we could not open, that Huang privately doubted his interim successor, CFO Colette Kress. The same report says Kress scaled back Nvidia's commitment to OpenAI's Ohio data center project: talks involved $250 billion in credit, but Nvidia committed $105 billion. These are anonymous-source claims relayed second-hand, and TechCrunch includes no Nvidia statement on them.

**Why it matters.** TechCrunch also quotes venture investors on what a more cautious Nvidia would mean. It cites PitchBook data showing Nvidia took part in 105 funding rounds this year, often as the largest investor. Umesh Padval of Seligman Ventures said, "When Nvidia was on the cap table, everyone felt safe." Dharmesh Thakker of Battery Ventures doubts that other large technology companies would write checks of hundreds of millions to startups that have no revenue yet. An unnamed investor said Kress is far more conservative. The article lists companies Nvidia has backed, among them CoreWeave, Crusoe (including a $1.4 billion Series E), Mistral AI, Lambda and Black Forest Labs, and says one investor described infrastructure startups raising about half of a GPU purchase from Nvidia at valuations of $1 billion or more. That is opinion from people with money at stake, not evidence of a change in policy.

**Business implication.** Startups that buy chips on Nvidia-backed terms should ask what happens to their financing if those terms change. The test comes Tuesday. Nvidia's calendar shows results at about 1:20 p.m. Pacific time and a call at 2 p.m., with written commentary from Kress published when the results go out. Listen for any language on investment commitments and on the OpenAI project.

### OpenAI Launches ChatGPT Wallet, Letting Its Agents Check Out for Pro Users

**What happened.** On Friday, TechCrunch reports, OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet. It lets OpenAI's agents, which it calls "dots," help a user pick items and finish the purchase. It starts with a limited number of U.S. ChatGPT Pro subscribers who are 18 or older and hold a U.S.-issued Visa, Mastercard or American Express card. A user adds a card once, reviews the items, the total and the card, then approves with Face ID or a fingerprint. The wallet charges the card with a payment token, which means the agent and the merchant never see the real card number. Purchases still earn card rewards and keep dispute rights, per TechCrunch. OpenAI says it plans to add spending limits and rules by category or time period, which would let users pre-approve some purchases, and to add more retailers. OpenAI also published a Commerce Partner Playbook for retailers. We could not open OpenAI's own page, which returned an access error, so the details above come from TechCrunch's description.

**Why it matters.** Until now agents could browse and fill a cart; the sale itself needed a person. A token and a biometric check sit in the gap. TechCrunch notes that the market is split. United Airlines said this week it would restrict outside agents on its own platforms, and Amazon has blocked agents and steers users to its own assistant, Alexa. DoorDash and Uber have reported low numbers of agent-placed orders, per TechCrunch, which also says Meta's Muse assistant has more than 10 million users and that AI-assisted shopping is a top activity there. That figure is Meta's, relayed second-hand.

**Business implication.** Retailers now have a choice: accept agent buyers on OpenAI's terms, build their own, or block them. The first launch has no named merchant partners in TechCrunch's account and no fees disclosed, so nobody outside OpenAI can yet say what this costs a seller. Payment companies are also affected, because a token-based flow keeps card networks at the center of agent commerce.

*Sources: [TechCrunch (Nov. 13)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/13/chatgpts-new-wallet-lets-ai-agents-shop-and-check-out-for-you/); OpenAI's announcement at [openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-wallet](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-wallet/), the link TechCrunch gives; our tools were blocked from it.*

### Thinking Machines Says Inkling 2 Small Weights Are Now Public

**What happened.** Thinking Machines Lab, the startup led by former OpenAI executive Mira Murati, announced Inkling 2 Small on Nov. 5. Its own page describes a "mixture-of-experts" model, one that splits its work across specialist sub-networks and uses only some of them for each word. It has 594 billion parameters in total, the numbers a model learns during training, and 17 billion active at a time. It accepts image input. At launch it was available through the company's Tinker platform with closed weights. A Nov. 12 update on the post says the weights are now on Hugging Face, the public model-sharing site. The original post planned an Apache 2.0 license, a permissive one, after outside safety testing and red-teaming. The update does not restate the license, and we did not see the Hugging Face listing, so confirm the terms before building on it. The post lists hosts including Nvidia, Fireworks, Together, Baseten, Nebius, Modal and Cloudflare.

**Why it matters.** All the figures below are the company's own claims, not independent results. It reports a score of about 31 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, 86.3% on SWE-Bench Pro v2 (Hard), a coding test, and 81.7% on ARC-AGI-2, a reasoning test. The page itself says it is not the best on every test: DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash scores higher on DeepSWE, 77.0% to 72.1%. It also says the model trails closed models such as GPT-6.1 Sol on alignment evaluations, and lists known weaknesses including wordiness and overreaction to some mental-health queries. That candor is useful, but it remains the author grading its own work.

**Business implication.** Open weights let a company run the model on its own hardware and tune it on private data, so data never leaves the building. That is the argument made against sending text to hosted vendors, a worry sharpened this week by the Fireworks incident in our last edition. Fireworks is one of the listed hosts; the post does not link the two events.

*Source: [Thinking Machines Lab, "Introducing Inkling 2 Small" (Nov. 5, updated Nov. 12)](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-small/). Every benchmark and cost figure is the company's own.*

### Democrats Ask Commerce to Preserve Records on the Nvidia China Chip Arrangement

**What happened.** According to TechCrunch, Rep. Richard Neal, the top Democrat on the House Ways and Means Committee, and Sen. Ron Wyden, the top Democrat on the Senate Finance Committee, wrote to Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick. They ask the department to preserve records about how Nvidia's chip sales to China were arranged. As the minority party they can ask questions but cannot compel answers, TechCrunch notes. The letter alleges that President Trump agreed to sales of Nvidia's H200 chips to China on condition that they first ship to the U.S., that a tariff was then added, and that Commerce then issued the export license. It calls this an "unconstitutional export tax system." It also alleges that Lutnick kept letting Megaspeed buy chips after intelligence officials warned him about its executive, and it asks whether $26 million in Nvidia donations to the president's projects were linked to the export deal. These are the letter's allegations, not findings. TechCrunch reports no Nvidia statement and no response deadline. We have not seen the letter itself.

**Why it matters.** The same article notes a separate, Republican-led probe by three House committees, to which Huang has agreed to testify. So both parties are now asking about the same chips, from different angles. The letter focuses on process and money; the Republican inquiry focuses on China.

**Business implication.** Chip buyers should expect more questions about where hardware ends up. A preservation request is early-stage: it precedes any finding, and the minority cannot force the department to comply.

*Source: [TechCrunch (Nov. 12)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/democrats-launch-nvidia-investigation-write-scathing-letter-on-huangs-scheme-with-trump/). We could not locate the letter's text.*

## Regulation and Policy Watch

**Asian governments react to Huang's exit.** TechCrunch compiled the responses. On Nov. 13 China's foreign ministry opposed export controls that disrupt global supply chains and said it was not aware of reports linking restricted Nvidia chips to a Shanghai data center tied to the former Megaspeed executive. TechCrunch, citing Reuters, says more than $3 billion in Chinese AI projects that rely on Nvidia's restricted processors face uncertainty. Singapore's police, the article says, found insufficient evidence of offenses over false declarations on server shipments in February but warned and barred the executive from re-entering, and its investigation into Megaspeed and six other individuals continues. South Korea's science ministry said Nvidia reaffirmed a plan to supply 260,000 advanced GPUs, and its AI strategy council met in an emergency session Friday. Taiwan's economy minister said the exit was "unlikely to significantly change the growth of the global AI industry," and Taipei's city government said Nvidia's overseas headquarters project remains on track. Japan's industry ministry said it will "closely monitor" the company. All of this is TechCrunch's compilation; we did not open the underlying ministry statements. *Source: [TechCrunch (Nov. 14)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/14/asian-governments-react-to-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huangs-exit/).*

## Emerging Startup Radar

**LangChain raises $300 million at a $3 billion valuation.** LangChain announced a Series C on Nov. 13. TechCrunch reports CapitalG led, with Singapore's GIC, DFJ Growth, IVP and Sapphire Ventures participating. A year earlier the company raised $125 million at $1.25 billion. LangChain began in 2022 as an open-source project that let early language models search the web and call other software. ## AI Infrastructure and Market Signals

**Google adds two TPU variants.** Press reports dated Nov. 9, among them The Next Platform, describe two new versions of Google's eighth-generation Tensor Processing Units, the chips it designs in-house: TPU 8tx and TPU 8i Max. The reports say the 8i Max puts two TPU 8i chips in one package for double the compute, memory cache and memory bandwidth of the 8i. Outlets disagree on what the 8tx is for, so we do not characterize it. Google's own page we could open covers only the original TPU 8t and 8i, announced April 22 at Cloud Next. The doubling claim is therefore not checked against a Google source. TechCrunch notes the launch came the same week that Nvidia's turmoil raised questions about its startup backing. *Sources: [The Next Platform (Nov. 9)](https://www.nextplatform.com/compute/2026/11/09/google-rejiggers-tpu-accelerators-for-broader-ai-inference-work/5308534); [Google, TPU 8t and 8i (Apr. 22)](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/infrastructure-and-cloud/google-cloud/tpus-8t-8i-cloud-next).*

## Forward-Looking Angle

Three things will answer questions left open today. Nvidia's call on Tuesday will show whether an interim chief commits to the same pace of investment and chip supply. The first weeks of ChatGPT Wallet will show whether users give agents their cards at all. And whether Hugging Face's Inkling 2 Small license matches the Apache 2.0 plan decides how freely companies can use it. Our reading, not a finding: the next stage of AI competition will turn less on who has the best model than on who controls payment, hosting and funding.
