## The Executive Read

The thread today is accountability for the things AI runs on and the things AI makes. Nvidia's board brought Jensen Huang back as chief executive and, in the same filing, created a committee on export controls and began a search for its first chief compliance officer. That came as Semafor reported that Commerce put Singapore's Megaspeed, the subject of chip-diversion allegations, on its trade blacklist. Three House committees and the Senate Banking Committee are lined up to question Nvidia. In the markets, TechCrunch reported that Anthropic's stock dropped below its $83 IPO price six days after it began trading, and that Nvidia shares gave back part of Monday's jump. In the labs, OpenAI published a preprint and lab results on GPT-6 designing proteins, and an essay arguing the industry should track one shared measure of model progress. Meta and Amazon are reportedly talking about letting Meta's Muse agent shop on Amazon again. Several claims today are the companies' own, and we label them. Nothing here is investment advice.

## Top AI Headlines

### Nvidia Reinstates Huang and Creates an Export-Control Committee as Megaspeed Is Blacklisted

**What happened.** On Nov. 23, Nvidia announced in a press release, filed with the SEC, that its board had unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as president, CEO and director, effective immediately. Nvidia announced Huang's resignation on Nov. 12, and on Nov. 17 board chair Harvey Jones said on an earnings call the board did not intend to bring him back. Nvidia now says the board received "input from senior levels of the U.S. government" and is confident his return poses no national security risk. The release also announces a new National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee of independent directors, and a search for a Chief Compliance Officer who would report to a board committee. Colette Kress, who was interim CEO, stays as chief financial officer. A separate board investigation into reports that Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort, announced Nov. 8, will "proceed to conclusion," the company says. The same day, Semafor reported that the Commerce Department added Megaspeed and six affiliates in Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand to its Entity List, which requires a license to sell them chips. Semafor first reported the plan, and its later article describes the listing as done. A Commerce Bureau of Industry and Security spokesperson is quoted saying exports of advanced chips to Megaspeed are now unlawful. We did not find the Federal Register notice.

**Why it matters.** Megaspeed is at the center of allegations that Nvidia chips reached China. A joint statement from the Justice and Commerce Departments dated Nov. 17 says Commerce began investigating Megaspeed in 2024, asked Nvidia for sales records in May 2025, and that Nvidia put Megaspeed on its own "no sell" list in June 2025. Agents conducted an in-person check at the company with Singaporean law enforcement that month. The Justice Department then took the lead on the criminal side. The statement announces no charges. Megaspeed has denied wrongdoing, TechCrunch reports. TechCrunch also reports that Attorney General Todd Blanche, at a Tuesday news conference, called Nvidia "a good corporate citizen" and suggested it is not a target, while declining to discuss the export investigation. The Wall Street Journal reported that Blanche told the president and vice president the department was not investigating Huang; TechCrunch relays that, and we have not seen the Journal's article. Separately, Reuters reported on Nov. 20 that the SEC is examining possible accounting issues at Nvidia, per people it did not name, according to TechCrunch. California's attorney general is also investigating. We have not seen those documents ourselves.

**Business implication.** Nvidia says it will keep cooperating with government inquiries. TechCrunch reports three House committees will privately interview Huang and Nvidia's top lawyer, Tim Teter, next week, and that the Senate Banking Committee will question Huang in public on Dec. 3. For customers, the practical effect is more paperwork: sales to data center operators in Asia may face deeper end-user checks. TechCrunch also reports that the U.S. issued rules last week barring large data centers that buy U.S. chips from serving customers in China and other countries of concern; we did not find that rule text.

*Sources: [Nvidia press release, Form 8-K (SEC), Nov. 23](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000090/nov23pressrelease.htm); [Nvidia board statement, Nov. 8](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/statement-from-nvidia-board-of-directors); [Justice Department, joint statement on Megaspeed](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/joint-statement-department-commerce-and-department-justice-megaspeed-investigation); [Semafor, Nov. 23](https://www.semafor.com/article/11/23/2026/commerce-to-add-megaspeed-to-trade-blacklist); [TechCrunch, Nov. 25](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/the-questions-jensen-huang-and-nvidia-will-still-have-to-answer-about-megaspeed/).*

### Anthropic Shares Fall Below the $83 IPO Price

**What happened.** TechCrunch reported on Tuesday that Anthropic's stock, which trades on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT, fell as low as $76 by midday, about 12% below Monday's close and under the $83 offering price. TechCrunch says the stock opened at $111.50 on Nov. 19, less than a week earlier. Anthropic's own release, dated Nov. 20, says the IPO closed at $83.00 a share, with 1,127,000,000 Class A shares sold, including 147,000,000 under the underwriters' option. Gross proceeds were about $93.5 billion, of which about $91.6 billion went to Anthropic. A concurrent private placement of 313,253,007 Class N shares to seven strategic investors added about $26.0 billion, for about $119.5 billion combined, before underwriting discounts and expenses. We have not verified live prices.

**Why it matters.** TechCrunch says trading under the offer price is often read as a negative signal, because underwriters usually price offerings below the level they expect the market to pay. It says venture investors view the stock as a barometer for other AI companies. Its article names no analysts or data providers, so treat that framing as the outlet's, and the price moves as its reporting of intraday trading.

**Business implication.** A new public company's first weeks are noisy, and one day's trading says little about its business. The more durable question is how this affects private AI companies weighing their own listings. We have no evidence yet on that.

*Sources: [Anthropic, IPO closing release, Nov. 20](https://www.anthropic.com/news/ipo-close); [TechCrunch, Nov. 24](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/anthropic-shares-fall-below-ipo-price-a-worrying-sign-for-ai-startups/).*

### OpenAI Reports Lab Results From GPT-6 Protein Design

**What happened.** On Nov. 24, OpenAI published a post and preprint saying its GPT-6 models can design protein structures and amino acid sequences using reasoning and general-purpose code, without calling specialized biology models. "Bound" means a designed molecule stuck to its target. The work with Onava, which produced and tested proteins in the lab, reports: 35 of 322 designed "minibinders" (small proteins) bound their targets, or 10.9%; 128 of 182 antibody-loop designs (70%) bound the IL-4Rα target; and 179 of 272 antibody-fragment designs (66%) bound PD-1 or PD-L1, two cancer-immunotherapy targets. On a motif-scaffolding benchmark, OpenAI reports its internal research model succeeded on 43.7% of tasks against 27.8% for RFdiffusion, a widely used protein design tool; it labels this preliminary. All of these are OpenAI's own numbers.

**Why it matters.** The post is candid about limits. Specialized tools were still used to filter candidates and assign sequences, so it does not show fully unaided design. Predicting a protein's structure from its sequence remains weak: an average TM-score (a structure-match measure where 1 is perfect) of 0.38 for the internal model, against 0.93 for the specialized ESMFold2. OpenAI also says some benchmark scores may reflect overlap with training data, that binding is only a first step before stability, activity, safety and efficacy, and that the capabilities could be misused. It says its Preparedness Framework guides that risk assessment. The post carries no independent review.

**Business implication.** The claim to watch is the interface, not the hit rate. OpenAI says its goal is to let scientists describe what they want a molecule to do in plain language. Drug developers will wait for outside labs to reproduce the results. OpenAI lists harder targets, effects such as killing cancer cells, and RNA and small molecules as next steps.

*Sources: [OpenAI, GPT-6 protein design (Nov. 24)](https://openai.com/index/gpt-6-models-demonstrate-intrinsic-protein-design-capabilities/).*

### OpenAI's Chief Scientist Proposes Perplexity as an Industry Yardstick

**What happened.** In an essay dated Nov. 22, "Perplexity and Intelligence," OpenAI's Jakub Pachocki argues that perplexity, a number measuring how well a model predicts the next word of text it has not seen, is the right shared measure of progress in pretraining, the first big training stage. OpenAI says it is publishing its own figures from a fixed snapshot of its internal codebase. It lists roughly 0.99 for GPT-3.5, about 0.91 for GPT-4 and about 0.76 for an upgraded model that produced results on open math problems; lower is better. It says capabilities in cybersecurity and math became transformative below 0.8, and expects further jumps in a window near 0.68 to 0.70. It adds that a sudden "avalanche" of pretraining advances is conceivable "as soon as 2027."

**Why it matters.** The essay proposes that developers jointly commit not to cross certain perplexity-based limits until monitoring tools mature, such as a successor to chain-of-thought monitoring, which reads a model's written reasoning. It also lists its own weaknesses: labs use different datasets so numbers do not compare cleanly, perplexity does not measure any specific task, and reasoning gains do not show up in it. Pachocki writes that current alignment auditing is expected to degrade, and that AI-assisted alignment is unlikely to keep pace. These are OpenAI's views and projections, not findings.

**Business implication.** A shared metric would give regulators and customers something to compare. No other lab has said it will adopt the proposal in anything we found.

*Sources: [OpenAI, "Perplexity and Intelligence" (Nov. 22)](https://openai.com/index/perplexity-and-intelligence/).*

## Model and Product Updates

**OpenAI and amateur astronomers: candidate planets.** OpenAI's Nov. 24 post describes two amateur astronomers using AI tools on public telescope data. Kevin Bond, an OpenAI engineer, used Codex on about 100,000 Kepler stars and flagged over 200 signals, 10 from already confirmed planets. Two are new candidates: KIC 5689121, with a dip every 13.8 days that could mean a planet about 2.4 times Earth's width, and KIC 6691030, a dip every 4.8 days. Ethan Byrd, founder of Primitive, used an AI tool the post calls Astra on about 19 million stars, mostly TESS data, and produced 49 candidates in a first pass. A SETI Institute astronomer independently found Bond's two signals. These are candidates, not confirmed planets, and confirmation could take months or years. Eclipsing stars and instrument effects can mimic planet dips.

*Sources: [OpenAI, "Searching for distant planets with Astra"](https://openai.com/index/searching-for-distant-planets-with-astra/).*

**Meta puts Muse in Instagram DMs.** Meta announced on Nov. 24 that its Muse assistant is rolling out inside Instagram direct messages on iOS and Android in the U.S., Canada, Mexico, Japan and Singapore. A save-and-organize prompt is U.S. and Canada only. Per TechCrunch, users can share posts with Muse and ask it to act, for example adding a café to an itinerary. Meta says "nothing is shared, published, or sent without their approval" and that users can ask Muse to forget information or undo actions. That is Meta's description.

*Sources: [TechCrunch, Nov. 24](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/meta-brings-its-muse-ai-assistant-directly-into-instagram-dms/).*

**Agent benchmark: ShellBench.** TechCrunch reports a new benchmark of everyday personal-agent tasks from researchers at the OpenClaw Foundation, Parsewave, Hugging Face, Red Hat and UC Berkeley, built on over 10 million anonymized real agent interactions. It says even top models from OpenAI, Anthropic and Meta failed more than a third of the time, and that 15.7% of attempts across all models failed at least one safety requirement. OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra scored highest but passed all three repeated runs of a task in under half of cases. Among the failures: 40.3% recommended duplicate or incorrect invoice payments. Results varied by "harness," the software wrapper around the model; Astra beat Claude Fable 5.2 in one and lost in Claude Code. The benchmark did not test models in their makers' own consumer apps. We have not read the paper.

*Sources: [TechCrunch, Nov. 24](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/even-the-best-ai-agents-struggle-with-everyday-life/).*

**Ling-3.2-air on Hugging Face.** The inclusionAI page lists Ling-3.2-air, a 128-billion-parameter model, as recently updated. An aggregator describes it as an Ant Group mixture-of-experts model with 5.5 billion parameters active per token under an MIT license; the model's own page did not show a license or benchmark results to us, so we cannot confirm either.

*Sources: [Hugging Face, inclusionAI](https://huggingface.co/inclusionAI).*

## Regulation and Policy Watch

**Congress and Nvidia.** The Senate Banking Committee's Dec. 3 hearing and next week's private House interviews, described above, are the first formal tests of the Megaspeed account. Per TechCrunch, Sen. Elizabeth Warren said Nvidia's executives and board "still owe Congress answers" and that the administration must explain what "input" officials gave on Huang's return. Republican committee chairs said they "remain committed to getting answers on the mass smuggling of Nvidia products." TechCrunch also reports House Foreign Affairs Chair Brian Mast asked Commerce for investigation data. Semafor reports Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Commerce controls the listing decision and that four other agencies had backed it for months.

**UK warns of faster AI-driven attacks.** TechCrunch reports the UK's National Cyber Security Centre described a "marked rise" in AI-driven attacks in recent months, with several incidents since September involving autonomous hacking tools. It names free open-source penetration-testing tools Artex and Cairn, released earlier this year. The NCSC's point, per TechCrunch, is that the flaws are old: weak passwords and exposed interfaces, now found in minutes. Its advice is that basic defenses still work. We did not locate the NCSC post itself, so the account rests on TechCrunch.

*Sources: [TechCrunch, Nov. 24](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/uk-warns-ai-driven-cyberattacks-are-exposing-years-of-weak-security/).*

**Pinterest adds parental AI limits.** TechCrunch reports Pinterest now lets parents limit how much AI-generated content teens see, calling it one of the first such options among major platforms. Pinterest's earlier AI-content settings, from 2025, did not fully remove AI content from feeds, per TechCrunch. This is platform policy, not law.

*Sources: [TechCrunch, Nov. 24](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/pinterest-will-now-let-parents-limit-the-ai-generated-content-their-teens-see/).*

## Emerging Startup Radar

**Maple, $5.3 million seed.** Per TechCrunch, Epic Ventures led the oversubscribed round, with Draper Associates, Planetary VC and Epoch VC. Maple, with four employees in Austin and San Francisco, sells a chat app that runs open models such as DeepSeek, Kimi, GLM and Gemma inside trusted execution environments, which are hardware-isolated areas of a server. It also launched Maple Work, a desktop agent app. Its pricing runs from $20 to $200 a month. The founders are CEO Mark Suman, a former Apple machine-learning engineer, and CTO Anthony Ronning. Maple says data is encrypted on the user's device with a password only the user knows, so it could hand over only encrypted data if subpoenaed. It reports 60 times growth over three months without disclosing user numbers. Those are company claims. Maple says it is completing SOC 2, a security audit standard, and plans a Series A in the first half of 2027.

*Sources: [TechCrunch, Nov. 24](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/maple-raises-5-3m-to-build-ai-products-that-protect-your-data-even-from-itself/).*

## AI Infrastructure and Market Signals

**Data center building gets harder.** A TechCrunch podcast episode says neoclouds, the smaller companies renting out GPUs, report construction delays and costlier debt, that Nvidia will not back a data center until it is close to operational, and that utilities are getting large customers such as TSMC to help fund new power delivery. It also says community opposition and state restrictions are blocking some sites and that labs and hyperscalers may build more of their own. The page gives no figures and the comments are from the episode's guests; treat them as industry opinion, not measured data.

*Sources: [TechCrunch podcast](https://techcrunch.com/podcast/ai-data-centers-are-becoming-harder-to-build-and-neoclouds-are-feeling-it/).*

**Amazon and Meta reportedly discuss Muse access.** TechCrunch, citing a Fortune report, says Amazon and Meta have held multiple rounds of talks on letting Muse connect directly to Amazon's store, which TechCrunch says users found blocked in September. Fortune reportedly says search-result presentation and advertising are unresolved, that Amazon is concerned about third-party shopping agents weakening its direct customer relationship, and that any deal needs both CEOs' approval. Neither company had responded to TechCrunch. We have not read Fortune's article, and nothing is agreed.

*Sources: [TechCrunch, Nov. 25](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/amazon-and-meta-reportedly-in-talks-to-lift-blockade-on-ai-agent-muse/).*
