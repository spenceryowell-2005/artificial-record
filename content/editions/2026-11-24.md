## The Executive Read

The thread today is who gets to say "yes" when software acts on its own. CrowdStrike, a security company, agreed to buy Zscaler for about $43.9 billion in stock, a deal its chief executive frames around securing AI agents. Anthropic released Claude Ship, which lets teams write a policy and have AI approve routine code changes without a person reading each one. OpenAI and Cisco released an open-source tool that lets AI-built patches shield a running Linux server before engineers can fix it properly. Meta opened its personal agent, Muse, to adults in the UK with a rule that every purchase needs the user's approval. And two U.S. states sued SpaceX, whose xAI unit builds Grok, alleging it did not do enough to stop its Grok image tool from producing harmful deepfakes (fake images of real people). Those are allegations in complaints that, per CNBC, had not yet been accepted by the courts when it reported; SpaceX disputes the framing. Amazon's Decart purchase, which we covered yesterday, now has more reporting behind it. Several beats had no verified news today, and we say which below. Nothing here is investment advice.

## Top AI Headlines

### CrowdStrike Agrees to Buy Zscaler for About $43.9 Billion in Stock

**What happened.** On Nov. 23, CrowdStrike announced a definitive agreement to acquire Zscaler in an all-stock deal that values Zscaler at about $43.9 billion. Each Zscaler share converts into 1.135 shares of CrowdStrike. CrowdStrike's release does not state a premium or a closing date. CNBC reports the deal is expected to close in the second half of CrowdStrike's fiscal 2028, which ends Jan. 31, 2028. CrowdStrike's chief financial officer, Burt Podbere, said the deal should be "significantly and immediately accretive to free cash flow per share upon close before factoring any synergies." (Accretive means it adds to earnings per share.) CrowdStrike reported $5.37 billion in cash at Oct. 31; the deal itself uses stock, not that cash.

**Why it matters.** Zscaler sells network security: it inspects and filters the traffic that moves between a company's people, apps and the internet. CrowdStrike's chief executive, George Kurtz, said the deal lets CrowdStrike "add the network, entering security's largest TAM as a scaled leader" (TAM is total addressable market, the size of the sales opportunity). The release's AI content is about CrowdStrike, not the deal: it says Falcon Guardian, its product for securing AI agents, has passed $100 million in annual recurring revenue. That figure is the company's own. CNBC reported that CrowdStrike shares fell 9% on Monday and Zscaler rose nearly 18%, and that JPMorgan analysts said the deal could broaden CrowdStrike's offering amid vendor consolidation. CNBC also lists comparable security deals: Google's $32 billion Wiz purchase, Cisco's roughly $28 billion Splunk and Palo Alto's $25 billion CyberArk.

**Business implication.** Security buyers have been consolidating vendors, and AI agents that can act inside company systems add pressure to do it. Customers of both firms should expect product-integration announcements, and regulators will review the deal. The release gives no leadership plan for the combined company.

*Sources: [CrowdStrike press release, Form 8-K exhibit 99.1 (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001535527/000153552726000035/crwd-20261123xex991.htm); [CNBC, Nov. 23](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/23/crowdstrike-to-buy-zscaler.html).*

### Anthropic Launches Claude Ship: AI Agents That Review, Fix and Approve Code Changes

**What happened.** Anthropic began rolling out Claude Ship on Nov. 23. It bundles three pieces for "pull requests," the proposals developers submit to change code. Code Review, which posts findings on a proposed change, is generally available. PR Steward, which works on failing automated tests, merge conflicts and review feedback, is in beta. Claude Approvals, also in beta, approves routine changes under a policy the team writes as a file in its code repository. Anthropic's page says PR Steward is designed not to approve or merge changes. It is available on Claude Team and Enterprise plans for GitHub repositories. It is not available to organizations that use zero data retention, the HIPAA configuration or customer-managed encryption keys. Developers start it with a `/ship` command in Claude Code.

**Why it matters.** The product moves the human from reading every change to writing the rule for which changes need reading. Anthropic recommends running Claude Approvals in "observe-only" mode first, starting with one repository, and setting spending limits. TechCrunch quotes Anthropic saying "most teams want a person to approve every change," and that Claude writes most of the Claude Code team's code. Both are Anthropic's statements; we have no independent measure of how well the approvals perform. Billing is by token, meaning usage. Claude Approvals has no base charge, but its classifiers are billed by use, and the page says a policy using Claude's review triggers is billed as Code Review even in observe mode.

**Business implication.** Cost will depend on pull-request volume and review depth, so teams should pilot before turning it on across a company. Anthropic is offering eligible Enterprise organizations at least $10,000 in credits, claimable until Jan. 15, 2027, which must be used within 30 days of claiming. Security teams will want to read the approval policy as carefully as they read the code.

*Sources: [Anthropic, "Introducing Claude Ship"](https://claude.com/resources/articles/introducing-claude-ship); [TechCrunch, Nov. 23](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/23/anthropic-launches-claude-ship-letting-ai-agents-review-fix-and-approve-code-changes/).*

### Oregon and Virginia Sue SpaceX Over Grok Deepfakes

**What happened.** Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield filed suit Sunday evening in Multnomah County Circuit Court, and Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones filed Monday morning in Richmond City Circuit Court, CNBC reported. CNBC says they are the first states to sue SpaceX over Grok, the image and chat tool built by xAI. Per CNBC, the states allege SpaceX lacked adequate safeguards against users creating and sharing harmful deepfakes, including suspected AI-generated child sexual abuse material, and falsely presented Grok and X as generally safe, in violation of Virginia's Consumer Protection Act and Oregon's Unlawful Trade Practices Act. They seek injunctions, civil penalties, disgorgement of profits and legal costs. Virginia also seeks restitution for consumers. CNBC reported the suits were awaiting court acceptance as of early Monday. We have not read the complaints or the dockets.

**Why it matters.** SpaceX told CNBC it takes "appropriate measures" against users who try to break its safeguards and that "attempting to create this material is a serious crime." CNBC also reports that in another case, a suit by the city of Baltimore against xAI, the company argued Section 230, the U.S. law that shields platforms from liability for user content, protects it. In a federal case in Texas, CNBC says SpaceX's lawyers called Grok "a neutral tool, subject to user control." CNBC lists other Grok-related probes and suits in the U.K., Spain, Ireland, France and California. It notes that state attorneys general recently won a settlement from Meta in which Meta agreed to pay about $17 billion over 10 years without admitting wrongdoing.

**Business implication.** State consumer-protection law gives attorneys general a route that does not depend on new AI legislation. Image-generation vendors should expect "were your safety claims accurate?" to become a standard line of questioning.

*Sources: [CNBC, Nov. 23](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/23/oregon-virginia-spacex-grok-deepfake-lawsuits.html). We could not locate the court filings; the account rests on CNBC's reporting.*

### OpenAI and Cisco Release Shirudo, an Open-Source Shield for Running Linux Servers

**What happened.** On Nov. 23, OpenAI and Cisco introduced Shirudo, a runtime that lets operators protect a running Linux system while patches are tested and deployed, without a restart. It uses eBPF, a Linux feature that runs small programs inside the operating system. A "shield" is a temporary block on the behavior an attacker exploits, while normal requests continue. Shields are cryptographically signed, and a hardware security chip, the TPM, supports a record of what was loaded or removed. OpenAI says that in a controlled test a shield generated by its Codex coding agent blocked an exploit attempt, called Java4Shell, against a running Elasticsearch service without a restart. The code is licensed BSD 2-Clause, with the BPF programs dual-licensed under BSD and GPL.

**Why it matters.** The pitch is speed: agents investigate a flaw, draft a shield and validate it, while security teams control what is deployed. That is OpenAI's own description and test; the post is not an independent evaluation. OpenAI lists a technical webinar on Dec. 15.

**Business implication.** Patching often means downtime, so a temporary block that can be monitored before it is enforced is useful to operators of older systems. Whether it works at scale is untested in public.

*Sources: [OpenAI, "OpenAI and Cisco introduce Shirudo" (Nov. 23)](https://openai.com/index/introducing-shirudo/); [OpenAI news index](https://openai.com/news).*

## Model and Product Updates

**Google DeepMind: Gemini 4 agent teams and quantum circuits.** In a Nov. 23 post, DeepMind says teams of Gemini 4 agents improved quantum algorithms, and that every result was proven correct in Lean, a formal proof checker. A quantum circuit is a program for a quantum computer. DeepMind's figures: a calculation for laser-driven fusion fuel became 11.8 times cheaper, and its memory need fell from 5,650 to 4,338 error-corrected qubits. A simulation for a light-sensitive molecule became about 8 times cheaper; one for the enzyme Cytochrome P450 became 2.5 times cheaper, with qubits falling from 481 to 333; and a routine called arcsin that loads numbers into quantum states became 290 times cheaper. These are the lab's own results. DeepMind says the circuits would still need more computing resources than the first error-corrected quantum computers are likely to provide, and it does not claim the simulations are practical today. It has open-sourced its Lean library and verified solutions. We read the blog post, not the arXiv paper it references.

*Sources: [Google DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/blog/finding-faster-quantum-algorithms-with-agents-and-formal-verification/).*

**Anthropic: Enterprise Frontier Safeguards in public beta.** Anthropic's page says the program watches for misuse patterns across multiple requests while customers keep the data in their own Amazon S3 or Google Cloud Storage. When it detects a risk, it opens a "Safety Case" for the customer's team to review, and cases do not contain inputs or outputs. Anthropic says neither it nor the cloud provider retains the data, staff do not review inputs and outputs, and the data is never used for training. It follows a private beta with more than 100 customers and is offered on Claude Platform, Claude Platform on AWS, Amazon Bedrock and Claude on Google Cloud. Microsoft Foundry is "coming soon." The Next Web reports the program is free apart from cloud storage costs and that customers must review cases within seven days or risk losing access; Anthropic's page did not mention the seven days, so treat that as The Next Web's reporting.

*Sources: [Anthropic, Enterprise Frontier Safeguards](https://claude.com/resources/articles/zero-data-retention-with-enterprise-frontier-safeguards); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/anthropic-enterprise-frontier-safeguards-zero-data-retention-beta).*

**Meta: Muse reaches the UK.** Meta says its personal agent, Muse, is now available to adults in the UK. It works through apps the user connects, such as email, calendar and shopping. Meta says it shows planned actions and waits for approval, and "purchases need your approval every time." A separate security agent checks Muse's actions against the permissions granted. Meta says Muse conversations are not shared with its ad systems, and that private messages such as emails and Instagram DMs are not used for model development. These are Meta's descriptions.

*Sources: [Meta, "Muse now available in the UK"](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/11/muse-now-available-in-the-uk-muse-does-the-work-you-stay-in-control/).*

## Regulation and Policy Watch

**State attorneys general move first on Grok.** See the lead item above. The practical point for policy watchers is the legal theory: both states argue deception about safety, not a violation of an AI-specific statute.

**Tidal bans fully AI-generated tracks.** Per TechCrunch, Tidal will begin removing fully AI-generated songs on Dec. 9 and expects to delete more than 29 million tracks by year-end. It reports that over the past 90 days more than 43% of tracks uploaded to Tidal were AI-generated, and that about 27 million tracks, 6.3% of the catalog, are fully AI-generated, 96% of which have no streams. Distributors may not deliver 100% AI-generated tracks; AI-assisted songs are still accepted. TechCrunch says Bandcamp banned AI music outright, Deezer is adding a filter, and Spotify and YouTube Music label it. These figures are Tidal's, as relayed by TechCrunch; Tidal's announcement is a post on X that we did not open. This is platform policy, not law, but it is the clearest line any major streaming service has drawn.

*Sources: [TechCrunch, Nov. 24](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/tidal-to-ban-and-remove-millions-of-ai-generated-tracks-as-ai-slop-inundates-music-streaming/).*

## Emerging Startup Radar

**Marlowe, $277 million seed at a $1.15 billion valuation.** Marlowe, founded in May and based in Bellevue, Wash., and Redwood City, Calif., builds AI computing capacity on AMD and Etched chips rather than Nvidia GPUs, according to GeekWire. Its co-founders are Dan Lewis, a Convoy co-founder and former Microsoft executive, and Kutta Srinivasan, a former Microsoft executive who led AI infrastructure. Accel led the round. Marlowe says its first site, on Etched chips, goes live in December, with a larger AMD Helios site targeted for the second quarter of 2027. It says it has signed three customer agreements but has not named the customers. It claims two to three times more output per watt and per dollar than conventional GPU clouds. That is the company's claim, and it has about 30 employees.

The vendor figures are the company's own. We did not find a Zipher press release; its website is the closest primary source.

*Sources: [TechCrunch, Nov. 24](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/why-a-crusoe-investor-is-betting-on-solar-and-batteries-to-power-future-ai-data-centers/).*
