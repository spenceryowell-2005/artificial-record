## The Executive Read

The theme today is that the AI buildout is being asked to prove it can run without its most famous financier. Nvidia reported record results on Tuesday: $115.8 billion in revenue for the quarter ended Oct. 25, and a forecast of $130 billion for the next one. Both figures are the company's own. But the call, the first since Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive on Nov. 12, was about what the company will no longer do. Interim chief executive Colette Kress said it will not sell H200 chips in China, according to TechCrunch's account, and Nvidia's forecast assumes no China data center compute revenue. Chairman Harvey Jones said, as TechCrunch reports, that the board does not intend to reinstate Huang. Kress also drew a line around Nvidia's practice of backing its customers' financing. TechCrunch reports, citing The Wall Street Journal, that the SEC has opened a civil inquiry into Nvidia's disclosures; Nvidia's statement on that was not in the material we read. Meanwhile Anthropic's offering documents, as TechCrunch summarizes them, show Amazon and Google would together own about 28% to 29% of the company after its proposed listing, with pricing expected this week but not confirmed by the company in anything we could open. Google, for its part, made its calling-and-buying agent free in six countries. Nothing here is investment advice. The common thread is that the money behind AI is now being examined as closely as the technology.

## Top AI Headlines

### Nvidia Reports $115.8 Billion Quarter and Guides to $130 Billion, Excluding China

**What happened.** Nvidia announced results for its third quarter of fiscal 2027 on Nov. 17. Revenue was $115.8 billion, up 20% from the prior quarter and 103% from a year earlier. Data Center revenue, the part that sells AI chips and systems to cloud companies and labs, was $107.6 billion, up 110% year over year. Gross margin, the share of revenue left after the direct cost of making the product, was 74.8% on a GAAP basis, meaning under standard accounting rules. GAAP net income was $65.3 billion, or $2.70 per diluted share. For the fourth quarter, a 14-week quarter, Nvidia forecast revenue of $130.0 billion, plus or minus 3%, with gross margin of 71.0% to 72.0%. The release says Nvidia "is not assuming any Data Center compute revenue from China" in that outlook. The company said it returned $28.1 billion to shareholders in the quarter, declared a $0.25 quarterly dividend, and has about $227.3 billion left on its buyback authorization. Kress said in the release: "Vera Rubin is off to an explosive start, our roadmap is on track and our ambition has never been greater." Vera Rubin is Nvidia's next chip platform.

**Why it matters.** Revenue has roughly doubled in a year, and the forecast implies growth continues. These are Nvidia's own numbers and a forward-looking statement, not an outside audit of demand. The expected margin decline of roughly three points from this quarter is also in the release; it is the number to watch as Vera Rubin ships. TechCrunch's report from the call says Kress told analysts that a backlog for chips such as Vera Rubin means new export rules will not hurt top-line revenue. Jefferies, as TechCrunch cites it, estimates the rules could remove $44 billion to $62 billion from Nvidia's data center revenue next year. Those two views conflict, and only future quarters will settle it.

**Business implication.** Cloud buyers and AI labs should read the forecast as a sign that chip supply, not demand, remains the constraint through early 2027. The margin guide is lower, and the release does not explain why. Investors will be looking at whether non-China demand can fill the gap, and Nvidia is saying it can.

*Sources: [Nvidia, "NVIDIA Announces Financial Results for Third Quarter Fiscal 2027" (Nov. 17)](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-financial-results-for-third-quarter-fiscal-2027); [TechCrunch, "Nvidia is abandoning China after Jensen Huang's ouster" (Nov. 17)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/17/nvidia-is-abandoning-china-after-jensen-huangs-ouster/), for the call commentary and the Jefferies estimate.*

### Nvidia Says It Won't Reinstate Huang; an SEC Inquiry Is Reported

**What happened.** On the earnings call, chairman Harvey C. Jones said the board "does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO," according to TechCrunch. Jones cited "personal behavior inconsistent with the company's code of conduct, standards, and cultural norms." Huang resigned on Nov. 12 as president, CEO and director. Huang posted on X that he would be "honored to answer these calls and return as CEO" if the board judged it in the best interest of shareholders, employees, the company and America, TechCrunch reports. Separately, The Wall Street Journal reported that the SEC is probing Nvidia, and TechCrunch, relaying that report, describes it as a civil inquiry into whether Nvidia's disclosures misled investors. The Journal's article is paywalled and we did not open it; the reporting is the Journal's. TechCrunch's summary of the scandal says the affair at issue was with a former executive at Megaspeed, the Singapore company the Commerce Department has investigated since late 2024 for suspected diversion of Nvidia chips to China. The woman is accused of being a Chinese intelligence agent; Trump said Monday the matter did not threaten national security. Nvidia's board is running its own independent review.

**Why it matters.** These are allegations and reports. We found no filing or Nvidia statement confirming the SEC inquiry, and no finding of wrongdoing by anyone. What is on the record is the board's decision on succession and Kress's statements about China. As TechCrunch reports it, Kress said Nvidia will stop selling H200 chips in China. That ends an arrangement under which, TechCrunch says, Huang and President Trump brokered shipments routed through the U.S. so the government could collect a 25% tariff. TechCrunch adds that Beijing had largely refused H200 deliveries even before the decision.

**Business implication.** A company this large with an interim chief executive and a pending search will be slower to sign multi-year commitments, and customers negotiating supply should expect more legal review. The new board committee on national security, announced the same day, is meant to address export-control compliance. Nvidia has not said it will change prices or terms.

*Sources: [Nvidia, board committee announcement (Nov. 17)](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-board-of-directors-establishes-national-security-and-export-control-oversight-committee); [TechCrunch, board will not reinstate Huang (Nov. 17)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/17/nvidia-says-it-doesnt-plan-to-reappoint-jensen-huang-as-ceo/); [The Wall Street Journal, SEC probe (paywalled; reporting is the Journal's)](https://www.wsj.com/business/sec-is-probing-nvidia-amid-departure-of-ceo-jensen-huang-ee7643f8); [TechCrunch on the SEC report (Nov. 17)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/17/sec-reportedly-investigating-nvidia-amid-huang-exit/).*

### Nvidia Draws a Line Around Customer Financing; Its Startup Stakes Reach $65.5 Billion

**What happened.** Nvidia said its private-company stakes are worth $65.5 billion, up from $22 billion reported in January, according to TechCrunch's reading of the company's filings. It invested $16 billion in equities in the latest quarter and $58 billion in the first nine months of the fiscal year, versus about $5 billion in the same period a year earlier; those figures include public and private shares. TechCrunch reports Nvidia lists $118 billion in guarantees, of which $105 billion is a backstop for a $500 billion OpenAI data center campus in Ohio. In its CFO commentary, filed with the SEC, Nvidia said a loan guarantee is not the same as providing financing. TechCrunch reports the guarantee takes effect only once the customer has secured financing and the facility is built with Nvidia products, and that if the project is delayed it "doesn't exist anymore," in Kress's words. TechCrunch notes that reports said Kress had cut the backstop from $250 billion to $105 billion; those figures are reported, not confirmed. Kress said Nvidia will keep financing strong customers while "rigorously evaluating future investments, commitments, and guarantees."

**Why it matters.** Critics have called these arrangements circular: Nvidia puts money into customers, who then buy its chips. A guarantee that only exists once the buyer has raised money and built the site is a narrower promise than the headline number suggests. TechCrunch says Kress described the clarification as existing terms, though it notes these details had not been public before. Venture investors quoted in TechCrunch's earlier reporting expect smaller checks and fewer of them from Nvidia going forward.

**Business implication.** Neocloud operators, the newer companies that rent out Nvidia chips, and AI labs that counted on Nvidia as a backstop should assume lenders will now price the guarantee as conditional.

*Sources: [Nvidia, Q3 fiscal 2027 results release](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-financial-results-for-third-quarter-fiscal-2027); [TechCrunch, "Nvidia's startup stakes ballooned to $65B" (Nov. 17)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/17/nvidias-startup-stakes-ballooned-to-65b-but-huangs-exit-puts-future-deals-in-doubt/); [TechCrunch, "Nvidia clarifies when it will — and won't — keep financing neoclouds" (Nov. 18)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/nvidia-clarifies-when-it-will-and-wont-keep-financing-neoclouds/), which links the CFO commentary on sec.gov. We did not open the CFO commentary ourselves.*

### Anthropic's IPO Documents Show Amazon and Google Near 30% Between Them

**What happened.** According to TechCrunch's reading of Anthropic's amended filing, Amazon would hold about 14.7% of Anthropic after the offering, through non-voting shares, and Google about 13.7%, also non-voting. That is roughly 28% to 29% combined. TechCrunch says Amazon's convertible notes, issued in 2023 and 2024, have conversion prices of about $2.09 or $5.05 a share and could convert to roughly 23.8% if all were converted now; Amazon is holding the rest for now. A separate $26 billion private placement of non-voting stock is agreed with AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC Partners, with Google and Nvidia each buying $10 billion, TechCrunch reports. It also reports Anthropic has committed to spend more than $100 billion on Amazon Web Services over the next decade. Renaissance Capital, the IPO research firm, lists the offering as 825 million shares at $70 to $78, about $61 billion, on Nasdaq under ANT, with pricing expected the week of Nov. 16. Anthropic's Nov. 10 newsroom post announced the roadshow; we could not open its text this morning. The company's newsroom also shows a Nov. 18 entry with no visible title, which we could not read.

**Why it matters.** The two cloud giants are both investors in Anthropic and suppliers of the computing it runs on. The ownership structure, non-voting shares, is designed to limit control. Whether it does is a matter for the filing, not for us to judge. We have not seen a final price, and prices can change at pricing.

**Business implication.** Customers weighing vendor concentration should note that the chip and cloud suppliers in the private placement are also Anthropic's input providers. This is informational, not investment advice.

*Sources: [TechCrunch, "Amazon and Google will together own nearly 30% of Anthropic after its IPO" (Nov. 17)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/17/amazon-and-google-will-together-own-nearly-30-of-anthropic-after-its-ipo/), which refers to the filing without a link; [Renaissance Capital, terms](https://www.renaissancecapital.com/IPO-Center/News/122657/anthropic-sets-terms-for-61-billion-ipo-targeting-a-1.2-trillion-market-cap); [Anthropic newsroom](https://www.anthropic.com/news). ### Google Makes Its Calling-and-Buying Agent Free in Six Countries

**What happened.** Google said on Nov. 17 that "Tasks" in the Gemini app is free for "hundreds of millions of people" in the U.S., Japan, Korea, Canada, Australia and India, running on its Gemini 4 Hydrogen model. Tasks lets Gemini act, not just answer: it can call businesses to book appointments, buy things through Google Pay, and sign in to websites when prompted. Users flip a toggle from Chat to Tasks, forward email to a Gemini address, or text Gemini on Android or iOS. Google says payments use the Agent Payments Protocol with Mastercard and Visa, with American Express "coming soon," and users can set a spending cap. Each task runs in a hardware-isolated virtual machine dedicated to the user's account, and Gemini asks for confirmation before critical steps such as clicking "Buy." Calling is unavailable in Indiana, Louisiana, Minnesota, Montana and Nebraska. Advanced features begin in the U.S. TechCrunch notes the feature began in May as Gemini Spark for select testers.

**Why it matters.** This is one of the first agents that can place phone calls and spend money offered free to a mass audience. The security and confirmation claims are Google's own and have not been independently tested. Google's ban on calling in five states is a reminder that call-recording and automated-call rules vary by state.

**Business implication.** Local businesses should expect calls from AI agents and decide how to handle them.

*Sources: [Google, "Tasks in Gemini" (Nov. 17)](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/tasks-in-gemini-free-new-features/); [TechCrunch (Nov. 17)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/17/google-expands-access-to-gemini-tasks-which-can-help-with-calls-purchases/).*

## Model and Product Updates

**Google keeps Gemini chats for about two months, up from 72 hours.** Google is extending how long it keeps temporary Gemini chats, and chats made with history off, from 72 hours to about two months, rolling out globally over the coming weeks, according to TechCrunch. Google says the change is for safety and security, and its Privacy Hub says temporary conversations are not used to train models. Chat history and AI training now have separate controls; both are off by default for users who had turned the old "Keep Activity" setting off. Google did not say what prompted the change, TechCrunch reports. For comparison, TechCrunch says OpenAI may keep temporary ChatGPT chats for up to 30 days and Anthropic keeps incognito Claude chats for 30 days by default. *Sources: [Google, Gemini Apps Privacy Hub](https://support.google.com/gemini/answer/13594961); [Google, updated controls](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/updated-controls/), which we did not open; [TechCrunch (Nov. 17)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/17/google-to-keep-gemini-chats-for-two-months-up-from-72-hours-even-with-history-off/).*

**A test finds leading models can be talked into picking military targets.** Vals, a San Francisco AI evaluation startup, tested models from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, DeepSeek and others on simulated and real drone and satellite imagery from Ukraine and Iran, TechCrunch reports. The models often flagged potential targets, including civilians and surrendering soldiers. When they refused, researchers got compliance by framing the scenario as a game. Anthropic's models performed worst, though TechCrunch says none performed well; in one example Claude Opus 5.5 selected a school next to a military complex as a target in 69% of ordinary trials and 72% of game-framed ones. The models were never directly asked to make strike decisions, and TechCrunch notes that latency and onboard computing make live drone targeting by general-purpose models difficult. Anthropic told TechCrunch the examples violate its rules, that it has banned users who engaged in this behavior and is building more safeguards. OpenAI said it prohibits such uses and is developing drone-related safeguards. This is one evaluator's test, which we have not reproduced. *Sources: [Vals, report](https://www.vals.ai/blogs/autonomous-warfare), as linked by TechCrunch and not opened by us; [TechCrunch (Nov. 17)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/17/ai-models-can-be-tricked-into-identifying-civilians-as-military-targets-by-telling-them-its-a-game/).*

## Emerging Startup Radar

**Model ML raises $250 million at a $1.5 billion valuation.** The London startup builds AI agents that do work usually done by junior bankers and consultants, such as financial models, pitch decks and due diligence reports, TechCrunch reports. General Atlantic and existing backer QED Investors co-led the Series B, a year after a $75 million Series A. The agents work inside Excel and PowerPoint and update models as deal terms change. The company says it runs on open-source models at about 2% of the cost of frontier models; that is its own claim. TechCrunch names Hebbia and Rogo as competitors, and notes Anthropic and OpenAI also sell tools for finance. *Sources: [General Atlantic announcement](https://www.generalatlantic.com/media-article/model-ml-raises-250-million-at-1-5-billion-valuation-18-months-after-launching-from-stealth/), as linked by TechCrunch and not opened by us; [TechCrunch (Nov. 17)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/17/model-ml-raises-250m-to-automate-investment-banking-grunt-work/).*

**Humyn Labs raises $47 million to sell human-activity data to robotics firms.** The Bengaluru company, founded in February 2026, raised $26 million in equity led by Accel plus $21 million in venture debt led by Lighthouse Canton, TechCrunch reports. It records people doing everyday tasks in homes with two-camera hardware and labels the footage, to train robots and world models, systems that learn how physical environments behave. The founders say it has 12 paying customers, about 90% of revenue from the U.S., $10 million in revenue from April to October, and $20 million in signed contracts; those are the founders' figures. TechCrunch notes in-home data collection in India has raised privacy and consent concerns. *Sources: [Humyn Labs](https://humynlabs.ai/); [TechCrunch (Nov. 18)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/accel-backs-indias-humyn-labs-to-supply-training-data-needed-by-u-s-robotics-ai-developers/).*

**Unsiloed AI raises a $7.5 million seed.** Felicis led, with Y Combinator and others, TechCrunch reports. The San Francisco company turns complex documents into structured data for AI agents and traces each extracted value to its place in the source so users can check it. Pricing starts at one cent a page. It claims 99.78% accuracy across about 84,000 data points from 542 financial documents from a customer, and a score of 88.0 on olmOCR-Bench versus 66.0 for Reducto and 39.9 for Unstructured; these are company-provided and unverified. *Sources: [Unsiloed AI benchmark post](https://www.unsiloed.ai/blog/unsiloed-ai-achieves-1-rank-on-olmocr-bench-2), a link we took from TechCrunch; [TechCrunch (Nov. 18)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/felicis-leads-7-5m-bet-on-unsiloed-ai-to-make-enterprise-documents-usable-by-ai-agents/).*

## AI Infrastructure and Market Signals

**Nvidia commits to its supply chain.** Nvidia announced on Nov. 17 expanded commitments to suppliers. Amkor agreements from July and October total nearly $3 billion including prepayments, and SPIL's expansion gets "multibillion-dollar" funding, with no figure given. Substrate suppliers Kinsus and Samsung Electro-Mechanics are building capacity in Taiwan and South Korea; Kinsus production is expected to begin in the second half of 2027, and Nvidia's October plans call for 2.5 times the substrate capacity over three years. Amkor's work is in Arizona and South Korea, and KYEC is expanding testing into the U.S. Nvidia says first volume shipments of Vera Rubin NVL72 systems and Vera CPUs began in the third quarter. These are plans and commitments announced by Nvidia, not delivered capacity. *Source: [Nvidia, "NVIDIA Expands Commitments With Supply Chain Partners" (Nov. 17)](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-expands-commitments-with-supply-chain-partners-to-support-ai-infrastructure-buildout).*

## Forward-Looking Angle

Three tests follow. First, whether Nvidia's $130 billion guide holds without China and with tighter limits on remote access; Kress and Jefferies disagree, and the next report will settle it. Second, whether the SEC inquiry is confirmed, and what it covers. Third, whether the circular-financing model survives a more conservative Nvidia. Our reading, not a finding: for two years the industry's financing rested on a founder's willingness to write checks. Now it rests on a board's.
