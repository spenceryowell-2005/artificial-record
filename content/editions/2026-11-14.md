## The Executive Read

Friday's news was about who gets to say no. Congress is moving to say no to chip exports, with OpenAI now reported to back the bills. An airline has said no to outside AI agents booking its flights. Four nonprofits have asked a California judge to say no to the next generation of frontier models, a request no court has acted on. And a breach at one AI hosting company showed what happens when a customer cannot say no to a vendor's weak link: Fireworks AI said attackers took billing records and some customer datasets, and Notion says chat transcripts from its AI feature were taken as well. Against that, Google shipped a new fast model, Gemini 4 Hydrogen, and priced it at $1.50 per million input tokens, according to its own announcement. SoftBank, meanwhile, has reportedly booked a charge of about $3.7 billion on power equipment it ordered for U.S. data centers and then revised. The common thread is friction. The industry's growth has depended on open doors: open chip channels, open vendor pipes, open websites. Those doors are now being narrowed, by lawmakers, by companies and by the plaintiffs' lawyers. Nvidia reports earnings Tuesday with an interim chief executive, and Anthropic's IPO is expected to price next Wednesday, according to press reports; the company has not confirmed that date. Nothing here is investment advice.

## Top AI Headlines

### Google Releases Gemini 4 Hydrogen, a Fast Model Priced at $1.50 per Million Input Tokens

**What happened.** Google published its announcement of Gemini 4 Hydrogen on November 12 and updated it November 13. Google calls it "our daily driver for agentic workflows," meaning software that carries out multi-step tasks on its own. It is rolling out globally in the Gemini API, Google AI Studio and Android Studio for developers, in the Gemini app and Google Search for consumers, and in Google's Antigravity coding tool for paid subscribers. Pricing is $1.50 per million input tokens and $7.50 per million output tokens, flat across a one-million-token context window. A token is a chunk of text, roughly three-quarters of a word. The model has four settings for how long it "thinks" before answering: low, medium, high (the default) and a new "xhigh."

**Why it matters.** Every benchmark figure below is Google's own claim, not independently verified. Google says Hydrogen scores 51 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at its default setting, against 50 for its larger Argon model, and 54 at xhigh. It reports 57% on Terminal-Bench 4.1, a test of command-line tasks, against 18.2% for the older Gemini 3.8 Flash, and says it finishes tasks about 2.5 times faster than that model. It also says the model ranks first on the Vals Tax Agent benchmark at 52.3%. The Index is run by a third party, but we have not checked its page. A "fast" model that scores close to the flagship is the pattern worth watching.

**Business implication.** For companies running agents at volume, the price per token matters more than a leaderboard position. Google has now put a near-flagship score at a fraction of the rate its flagships carry, though we have not compared prices against the other Gemini 4 models and the announcement does not say how long this price will last. Developers should test it on their own tasks before trusting the vendor chart.

*Sources: [Google, "Introducing Gemini 4 Hydrogen" (Nov. 12, updated Nov. 13)](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-gemini-4-hydrogen/). All performance and pricing figures are Google's.*

### Congress Moves on AI Chip Export Limits as New Research Traces Banned Chips in Chinese Models

**What happened.** Semafor reported Thursday that three bills to tighten AI chip exports are in the Senate's version of the annual defense bill, but there is no deal to include them in the final text. They are the Chip Security Act, which targets smuggling partly by verifying where chips end up; the MATCH Act, which closes loopholes on chipmaking equipment; and the AI Overwatch Act, which would treat chip sales more like foreign arms sales and give Congress a say over some Commerce Department licences. Senate Majority Leader John Thune told Semafor there is a "high probability" Congress adds restrictions. Anthropic publicly supports all three, Semafor says, and OpenAI "has warmed to them." Bloomberg reported separately that OpenAI told lawmakers it backs the effort; we could not open Bloomberg's article and rely on its headline and Semafor's description. The Information Technology Industry Council, a trade group, opposes the bills, saying they would "undermine the American tech stack."

Separately, TechCrunch reported Friday on an analysis by Will DePue, a former OpenAI researcher who leads the nonprofit Unemployment Labs. He examined the published weights of open models from DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, MiniMax and Ant Group and reportedly found "fingerprints from training on Blackwell chips" in Moonshot's Kimi K3 and DeepSeek's V4 releases. Blackwell is Nvidia's current top chip line and is barred from export to China. TechCrunch notes this does not show the models were trained only on those chips, or that any law was broken, because the chips could sit in offshore data centers. It cites Epoch AI estimates of about 275,000 Nvidia accelerators imported to Malaysia in 2024 and 2025. We have not seen DePue's methods or code, and the article carries no response from the four Chinese companies.

**Why it matters.** The political trigger, per Semafor and TechCrunch, is the scandal around Nvidia's former chief executive, Jensen Huang, who resigned Thursday. Semafor reports that Nvidia sold $4 billion of chips to Megaspeed, a Singapore company suspected of diverting them to China, and that the Justice Department is investigating. Those are press accounts; our November 12 edition covered Nvidia's own statement. Semafor adds that the bills could be caught up in a fight over the December funding deadline, and that Democrats take control of both chambers next year.

**Business implication.** If any of the three passes, chip buyers outside the U.S. and its close allies would face more paperwork and more checks on where hardware goes. The measure to watch is the final defense bill text, not the Senate's version.

*Sources: [Semafor](https://semafor.com/article/11/12/2026/a-high-probability-congress-pushes-tighter-export-controls-following-nvidia-scandal); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/13/export-banned-nvidia-chips-are-behind-chinas-most-powerful-ai-models-uh-oh-jensen); [Bloomberg, "OpenAI Backs US Legislation to Restrict China's Access to AI Chips" (paywalled; reporting is Bloomberg's)](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/openai-joins-groundswell-of-support-for-ai-chip-control-measures). The bill texts were not reviewed.*

### Four Nonprofits Sue OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta and SpaceXSI to Halt More Capable Models

**What happened.** According to the Associated Press, four nonprofits filed suit in San Francisco Superior Court on November 12: the Irreplaceable Education Fund, Public Citizen, Heat Initiative and Alliance for Secure AI. The defendants are Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI and "SpaceXSI." The AP says the complaint alleges violations of California's unfair competition law by endangering public health and interfering with property, and asks a judge to stop the companies developing or deploying new AI models. Other reports describe two requested orders: one barring systems more capable than each defendant's most capable system as of the filing, and one barring AI from autonomously directing research on more capable AI. We have not seen the complaint, so those details rest on press accounts. The companies did not immediately respond to requests for comment, per the AP. No hearing date has been reported.

**Why it matters.** The AP says the complaint leans on the labs' own public warnings that their technology could someday "destroy humanity." That makes the companies' safety statements into evidence against them. A separate antitrust case filed in September, which we did not review, alleges that rival labs illegally agreed to slow down.

**Business implication.** Courts rarely grant orders this broad, and nothing here has been tested. The practical risk for the labs is discovery: if the case survives early motions, internal safety documents could be requested.

*Sources: [Associated Press via News4Jax (Nov. 12)](https://www.news4jax.com/business/2026/11/12/advocacy-groups-sue-ai-giants-to-freeze-new-models-warning-of-catastrophic-risks/). We could not locate the docket or complaint; the allegations are the plaintiffs' and unproven.*

### Fireworks AI Says Attackers Took Billing Data and Some Datasets; Notion Says AI Chat Transcripts Were Exposed

**What happened.** Fireworks AI, a platform that hosts open AI models for developers, updated its security bulletin on November 9. It says a third-party cloud provider that hosted its research machines had a security incident on October 23, and on October 24 an unauthorized party used that to reach Fireworks development machines and run an unauthorized workload. Fireworks says the data reached includes resource metadata, billing and shipping addresses, invoice and payment history, account balances and credit usage. It says payment card numbers were not stored and were not affected. For a limited set of accounts, it says secrets, datasets and some incomplete model weights were accessed. It says its inference product, Training SDK and Nexus show no impact; all three keep no customer data by default. Fireworks says it notified law enforcement and engaged Palo Alto Networks' Unit 42 for forensics, and that the investigation continues. It links the activity to a group using "LLMjacking," a tactic of reselling stolen access to AI models, citing a November 2 post from Google Threat Intelligence Group and Mandiant.

Notion's own disclosure says it cut its systems off from Fireworks on November 3 and that Fireworks deleted Notion's data on November 4. The exposed data is chat transcripts from Notion AI's "Insights" feature and inference, which may include names, emails or API tokens that users typed. Notion says fewer than 1% of workspaces were affected and that most transcripts lacked data tying them to a user or workspace. TechCrunch reported this is Fireworks' second incident in two months.

**Why it matters.** The weak point was a vendor in the middle. Many software products send customer text to outside AI hosts, and each host may hold a copy of whatever users typed.

**Business implication.** Notion has turned off Insights during the investigation, stopped serving open-weight models through Fireworks and says it is auditing vendors for zero data retention, meaning the vendor keeps no copy. Buyers can ask the same of their own AI suppliers. Notion also tells affected owners to rotate any credentials that appeared in chats.

*Sources: [Fireworks AI security bulletin (updated Nov. 9)](https://fireworks.ai/security/bulletins/october-2026-security-incident-update); [Notion, security incident disclosure](https://www.notion.com/help/security-incident-involving-a-third-party-ai-vendor); [TechCrunch (Nov. 12)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/cyberattack-on-fireworks-ai-allowed-hackers-to-access-notion-customers-chat-transcripts/).*

### SoftBank Reportedly Books a $3.7 Billion Charge on U.S. Data Center Power Equipment

**What happened.** SoftBank Group reported fiscal second-quarter results on November 10. Press accounts agree on a charge of about ¥597.2 billion, roughly $3.7 billion, for revising procurement of power-generation equipment for U.S. AI data center projects, including a campus in Pike County, Ohio. The reports describe it as a write-down of nonrefundable advance payments. Accounts of the headline result differ: Mobile World Live and investingLive report a net loss of about ¥1.1 trillion for the quarter, against a ¥2.5 trillion profit a year earlier, while XTB reports a profit of ¥347.3 billion. We could not read the earnings figures in SoftBank's own report and cannot say which is right. investingLive says SoftBank's OpenAI stake rose to about 13% after a further ¥3 trillion investment, and that any gain from OpenAI's new funding round is not yet in the results.

**Why it matters.** Last week's Anthropic generator story, reported by the Financial Times, was about a lab buying power equipment early. This is the other side: a buyer that ordered and then changed its plans, and lost the deposits. SoftBank cited evolving project requirements, according to the reports.

**Business implication.** Power equipment has long lead times, so orders go in years before data centers are finished. If demand forecasts move, the deposits are the exposure. That is our reading, not SoftBank's statement.

*Sources: [SoftBank Group, Q2 FY2026 results briefing page (Nov. 10)](https://group.softbank/en/news/webcast/20261110_02); [investingLive](https://investinglive.com/stocks/softbank-shares-down-after-swings-to-7-billion-q2-loss-on-3-7-billion-us-data-centre-power-hit/); [Mobile World Live](https://www.mobileworldlive.com/?p=517624). We could not extract figures from SoftBank's financial report PDF; all numbers are from press accounts.*

## Model and Product Updates

**OpenAI launches security products.** The Register reported Thursday that OpenAI launched two products under its Daybreak security platform. Red Team, in early access, uses fleets of agents to test web applications for weaknesses and to validate findings from other scanners. Defense Factory finds, validates and drafts fixes for vulnerabilities, using OpenAI's GPT-6 Cyber model; engineers review each patch before it ships. OpenAI's Nancy Wang called Red Team "superhuman pen-test at the scale of automated continuous scanning," which is the company's claim, not a measured result. We could not open OpenAI's own pages, so we link The Register. *Source: [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/11/12/openai-launches-security-products-tells-orgs-theyre-doomed-if-they-dont-use-them/5309500).*

**IBM tests a defense against prompt injection.** IBM released two experimental Granite 4.1 variants, 3 billion and 8 billion parameters, trained on a markup format called LLMON. It separates instructions from the data a model reads, such as a web page, so hidden commands in that data are ignored. Prompt injection is the trick of hiding such commands. IBM says on one benchmark the approach avoids 47% more attacks than the best frontier model it tested. IBM's authors say the previews are "not for production," that the results are their own evaluations, and that the separation is "a learned behavior, not an enforced guarantee." *Source: [IBM on Hugging Face (Nov. 12)](https://huggingface.co/blog/ibm-granite/llmon-an-llm-native-markup-language-now-in-granite).*

**United restricts outside AI agents.** United Airlines announced a policy against "unauthorized, third-party personal AI agents or assistants" booking or changing flights on its own platforms and through travel agencies, according to Skift. United cited account-login security, unintended purchases and FAA and DOT rules. Skift, citing The Wall Street Journal, quotes CEO Scott Kirby: "I don't want to create another middleman." The reporting on the quote is the WSJ's. The policy reportedly still lets agents display flights and prices; we have not seen United's contract text. *Source: [Skift (Nov. 11)](https://skift.com/2026/11/11/united-airlines-moves-to-block-personal-ai-agents-from-booking-flights/).*

**Google and Samsung open preorders for Gemini glasses.** The Next Web reports nine styles from Warby Parker and Gentle Monster priced $395 to $489, shipping December 10, with a 12-megapixel camera and about nine hours of battery. It says bystanders' faces are blurred before Gemini processes an image. Those are the outlet's description of company details. *Source: [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/google-samsung-smart-glasses-preorders-warby-parker-gentle-monster).*

## Regulation and Policy Watch

**FAA investigates whether its new AI traffic tool played a role in a nationwide ground stop.** A communications failure at air traffic control centers triggered a nationwide ground stop Friday, according to KPRC in Houston. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said DHS is working with other agencies, through its cyber agency CISA, to determine whether the FAA's new AI system contributed, and called it a telecommunications issue with no threat to the public. The system is SMART, which the Transportation Department describes as a tool that uses real-time data on weather, congestion and flight paths to suggest routes; FAA staff review its recommendations. KPRC says rollout began in September and that officials have not established that the AI caused the problem. A later KPRC item says the stop was lifted, with delays expected. *Sources: [KPRC/Click2Houston (Nov. 13)](https://www.click2houston.com/news/local/2026/11/13/faa-investigating-whether-new-ai-technology-contributed-to-nationwide-ground-stop/); [Transportation Department, SMART fact sheet](https://www.transportation.gov/sites/dot.gov/files/2026-09/SMART%20Fact%20Sheet.pdf), which we could not open.*

**Nvidia and the Hugging Face deal.** TechCrunch reports Nvidia's planned $12.9 billion purchase of Hugging Face, announced in September, is still scheduled to close in the first half of 2027, and that the Justice Department began asking about its competitive effects, per an October Reuters report we have not seen. Hugging Face CEO Clément Delangue called Huang's exit "a really sad day" and said the company will push harder for open-source AI. Requests for comment on the deal's status went unanswered. *Source: [TechCrunch (Nov. 12)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/hugging-face-ceo-vows-to-keep-fighting-for-open-source-ai-after-jensen-huangs-exit/).*

## Emerging Startup Radar

**Oura agrees to buy Counsel Health.** Oura, the smart-ring maker, announced a definitive agreement on November 10 to acquire Counsel Health, an AI primary-care company whose service lets users message a chatbot and bring in a physician. The two have partnered since May. Oura's post does not give a price; MedTech Dive reports $625 million in cash and says it expects the deal to close by year's end. Oura CEO Tom Hale said, "Together, Oura and Counsel can bridge that gap," meaning between health data and care. Counsel's founder, Muthu Alagappan, said physicians stay "in the loop for clinical decision-making." *Sources: [Oura](https://ouraring.com/blog/oura-to-acquire-counsel-health/); [MedTech Dive](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/oura-makes-bet-on-ai-in-healthcare-with-625m-counsel-health-buy/874398/).*

**millionways, $6 million.** The New York startup launched Thorsten-4, a model trained on psychologist-labeled interviews of more than 7,000 people, aimed at inferring motivation from text, with sales and executive hiring as the suggested uses. The founders say it adds "a level of human understanding" that general models lack. In a Semafor reporter's test, it reached a more confident reading of an interview than Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.2 did. That is one test, not a benchmark. *Source: [Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/11/11/2026/new-ai-model-from-startup-millionways-wants-to-read-between-the-lines).*

**Norwest, $4 billion fund.** TechCrunch reports the 65-year-old venture firm closed its 18th and largest fund, with plans to expand into physical AI, voice AI, agentic cybersecurity and aerospace. Partner Jeff Crowe said, "We're taking a broad approach to AI because its impact is broad." *Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/norwest-pushes-deeper-into-ai-with-new-4b-fund/).*
