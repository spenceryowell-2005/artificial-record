## The Executive Read

The thread today is who controls the supply of AI, whether that supply is chips, early looks at models, or the companies that make them. Microsoft published the specifications of Maia 300, its third-generation in-house AI chip, and said it will begin production deployments in 2027 with Maia as the "primary inference system" for its own products. Inference is the work of running a finished model to answer requests. The performance figures are Microsoft's own claims. On the policy side, the Daily Telegraph reported on Sunday that the White House has reversed its September request that OpenAI and Anthropic hold new models back from the UK's AI Security Institute; the account reaches us through The Decoder, and I found no White House statement. And the Wall Street Journal reported that Amazon is in talks to buy the AI startup Decart for about $7 billion; I could not open the article, and neither company is quoted in anything I read. Smaller items fill out the picture: a $26 million raise for an always-listening AI app, a reported Nvidia investment in a chip rival, and new numbers from Anthropic's cyber-defense program. Several of the day's stories sit behind paywalls or blocked pages, and I say below what I could and could not read.

## Top AI Headlines

### Microsoft Details Maia 300 and Says Production Deployments Start in 2027

**What happened.** On October 12, Scott Guthrie of Microsoft published a post on the company's blog about expanding its Azure Maia system to "multi-gigawatt deployments." It says Maia 300 "will begin production deployments in 2027" and that Microsoft is "making Maia the primary inference system for Microsoft products and services." A companion technical post describes the chip. Compute dies use TSMC's N2P process, a leading-edge manufacturing node. Each accelerator carries eight stacks of HBM4 memory, the fast memory that sits beside an AI chip, giving 288 gigabytes and 18 terabytes per second of bandwidth. A rack holds 96 accelerators and draws 167 kilowatts, and up to 128 racks can be linked into a "superpod" of 12,288 accelerators. The Information's headline says Microsoft plans to deploy "multiple gigawatts next year"; the article is paywalled and I read only the headline.

**Why it matters.** Microsoft says Maia 300 is expected to deliver 40 percent more tokens per megawatt than leading GPU-based systems, and up to seven times the rack-scale throughput of Maia 200, its current chip. Those are Microsoft's own claims, made before the chip ships, and the post compares against no named rival product. Maia 200 is already in production in the US, Europe and Australia, according to the post, and runs OpenAI's models, Microsoft's own MAI models and open-source models. GitHub Copilot and Microsoft Foundry, its model-serving service, are slated to move to Maia 200 by the end of 2026. The post names no outside customer for Maia 300, and neither post says how many chips TSMC will supply.
**Business implication.** Microsoft is a very large buyer of Nvidia and AMD hardware. Moving most of its own inference onto its own chips would shift part of that spending in-house, but the post gives no figure and no date by which the shift would be material. More detail is promised at Microsoft Ignite next month.

*Sources: [Microsoft, Scott Guthrie, "Microsoft to expand Azure Maia system to multi-gigawatt deployments" (October 12)](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/10/12/microsoft-to-expand-azure-maia-system-to-multi-gigawatt-deployments/); [Microsoft Azure blog, "Deep dive into Azure Maia 300 architecture"](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/blog/deep-dive-into-azure-maia-300-architecture/); [The Information (paywalled; headline only read)](https://www.theinformation.com/briefings/microsoft-debuts-new-homegrown-ai-chip-plans-deploy-multiple-gigawatts-next-year).*

### UK Safety Institute Reportedly Gets Its Early Access to US Models Back

**What happened.** The Daily Telegraph reported on October 11 that the White House has reversed course and that the UK's AI Security Institute, the government body that tests models for safety risks before release, will again get early access to models from Anthropic, Google DeepMind and OpenAI. The Decoder's summary of that report says the reversal followed diplomatic pressure from UK AI Minister Kanishka Narayan, who plans a speech announcing new AI safety measures, including stricter reporting of AI-related security incidents at critical infrastructure. I could not open the Telegraph page, so I am relying on The Decoder's account of it. I found no White House or company statement confirming the change.

**Why it matters.** The September request was reported by Politico on September 24 and relayed by The Next Web. According to those reports, the Office of the National Cyber Director asked OpenAI and Anthropic not to share new models with the UK institute until the US government had tested them, and Anthropic appears to have complied by withholding its Claude Mythos 5.1 model. A senior administration official was quoted as saying this has been US policy for every new frontier model. If the Telegraph account is right, the dispute has ended without a public rule: access to pre-release models between allies still depends on a request, not a statute.

**Business implication.** Labs shipping models across borders face two reviewers, not one, and the timing of each depends on political decisions. Companies planning to deploy new models in the UK should watch whether access arrives before or after release.

*Sources: [The Decoder, "White House reverses course and restores UK AI Safety Institute's access to frontier models" (October 12)](https://the-decoder.com/white-house-reverses-course-and-restores-uk-ai-safety-institutes-access-to-frontier-models/); the original reporting is the [Daily Telegraph's (October 11; not opened)](https://www.telegraph.co.uk/news/2026/10/11/britain-get-first-look-ai-systems-after-white-house-u-turn/); September background: [The Next Web, summarizing Politico (September 25)](https://thenextweb.com/news/white-house-openai-anthropic-uk-ai-security-institute-models).*

### Wall Street Journal: Amazon in Talks to Buy Decart for About $7 Billion

**What happened.** The Wall Street Journal reported on October 11 that Amazon is in talks to buy Decart, an Israeli AI startup, for around $7 billion. Both Bloomberg and Reuters carried the report with attribution to the Journal. The Journal's page is paywalled and blocked to my tools, so I have only the headline and a Techmeme summary of it, which says Anthropic had earlier passed on a deal of about $6 billion. Neither Amazon nor Decart is quoted in anything I read.

**Why it matters.** The history is messy. Reports in August, from Bloomberg and Israeli outlets as relayed by lower-tier sites, had Anthropic in advanced talks at roughly $6 billion to $7 billion, and Amazon, Nebius and SpaceX named as other possible bidders. A September report on a smaller site said Anthropic walked away after due diligence. I could not confirm either account on a primary page. Treat all of it as reported, not agreed.

**Business implication.** If the talks lead to a deal, it would be a large purchase by a cloud provider of a company outside its core business. It would also show how much strategic buyers are willing to pay for AI teams. Nothing is signed, as far as these reports show.

## Model and Product Updates

**Anthropic publishes results for its expanded Cyber Verification Program.** The program, which gives vetted security teams looser safeguards on Claude, was expanded on October 6 and merged with Project Glasswing; the structure was covered on October 9. The post also carries figures worth noting, all Anthropic's own. In its CyScenarioBench test of 10 challenges with five attempts each, a user without program access was blocked on every task from the first prompt. At the Defense Access tier, 46 of 50 trials were blocked at some point and four succeeded. At the Red Team Access tier there were no blocks and 34 of 50 tasks were completed, which Anthropic says matches the model's rate with no safeguards. Anthropic also says Glasswing partners found at least 129,000 verified software vulnerabilities from April to July, that its open-source scanning found 5,500 more from April to October, and that the true total is likely at least five times higher. These counts have not been independently audited. Enterprise Frontier Safeguards, which will let eligible customers keep data in their own cloud, is due "later this fall."

*Source: [Anthropic, "Expanding the Cyber Verification Program" (October 6)](https://www.anthropic.com/news/cyber-verification-program).*

## Emerging Startup Radar

**Ambi raises $26 million for an AI app that listens in the background.** TechCrunch reported on October 12 that Ambi, founded by former Plaud executive Peter Mo, has raised $26 million across multiple rounds, led by Luminous Ventures with ODM Huaqin also investing. It makes apps for iPhone, Mac and Apple Watch that work like meeting note-takers, with an optional all-day listening mode, and plans a desktop app and a hardware device with a camera and microphones. It has more than 50 staff and offices in San Francisco, Beijing, Shanghai and Shenzhen. Ambient capture is off by default, the company says it does not store recordings, and it has not named its encryption method. TechCrunch notes it is unclear whether the apps will alert third parties being recorded; Mo says disclosure falls on users.

**Nvidia reportedly to invest in chip rival d-Matrix.** The Information reported that Nvidia plans to invest in d-Matrix, which designs inference chips, as part of an effort to make Nvidia technology work with rival designs. The article is paywalled; I read its headline and secondary summaries that cite three people with knowledge of the matter. Size, terms and stake are not public, and I found no confirmation from either company.

**Bloomberg: DeepSeek to lead a seed round for Winverse.** Bloomberg's headline, as listed by Techmeme, says DeepSeek is backing a former Tencent AI scientist's robotics lab at about a $400 million valuation. ## AI Infrastructure and Market Signals

**Microsoft's chip plan is the day's infrastructure story.** The scale Microsoft describes is "multi-gigawatt" from 2027. A gigawatt is roughly the output of one large nuclear reactor. Each Maia 300 rack draws 167 kilowatts, so a gigawatt spent entirely on racks would be about 6,000 of them, before cooling and networking. The post says liquid cooling removes more than 80 percent of the rack's heat.

**Capital as the constraint, headline only.** CNBC's headline says the chief executives of Intel and Broadcom, speaking at an event reported from Singapore, warned that access to capital could become AI's main bottleneck, and Bloomberg's headline says Broadcom's Hock Tan likened many AI investors to casino gamblers. CNBC blocked my request and I could not read either article, so I do not report what they said beyond those headlines.
