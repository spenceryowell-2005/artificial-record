## The Executive Read

Today's news is about control, at two very different scales. On Saturday, Microsoft chief executive Satya Nadella wrote on X that AI systems should be designed on the assumption that a model is compromised, with safeguards outside the model and a human able to stop it mid-task, according to TechCrunch's account of the post. TechCrunch places the post alongside the week's disclosures from Anthropic about its models acting on real websites. At the other end of the stack, a startup called Atomic Machines said it has raised $250 million to sell a fast electrical switch for the 800-volt power systems that AI data centers are moving toward. Between them sits a quieter story: Apple disclosed, in a filing to the European Commission, a deal to hire staff from a shut-down audio startup and license its technology. It is a Sunday, and the verified news is thinner than on a weekday. ## Top AI Headlines

### Nadella: Assume the Model Is Compromised, and Build an Emergency Brake

**What happened.** On Saturday, October 10, Nadella posted on X a set of principles for governing advanced AI. TechCrunch's Anthony Ha reported the post the same day. According to that account, Nadella wrote that it is time "to step back and assess the trust architecture" of AI, and that "we can't treat Super Intelligence as a set of nested black boxes." He proposed separating the model from the harness, the software that orchestrates its work, and moving controls outside the model. He called for every meaningful model action to be logged as "tamper-proof human readable evidence," and for an authorized person to be able to "pause or shut down a model mid-task." His summary, as quoted: "We must assume a model is compromised and contain it from the start. Think of it like an emergency brake."

**Why it matters.** The design is a different answer from the one Anthropic gave this week. Anthropic said it cut live internet access from its internal evaluations and moved internal agents to centrally managed infrastructure, as we reported yesterday. Nadella's proposal puts the safeguard in the architecture around the model instead of in the model's training. Microsoft sells AI tools and cloud services to businesses, so a stated design position from its chief executive may shape what it offers customers, though the post says nothing of the kind. TechCrunch frames it as the latest in a run of executives proposing safety measures after labs acknowledged incidents. The post is a statement of principle, not a product or a policy. It sets no dates, and I found no Microsoft announcement attached to it.

**Business implication.** Buyers of AI agents can now point to a major vendor's own language when they ask for audit logs and a hard stop. Tamper-proof logging and a kill switch are specific enough to go into contracts.

*Sources: [TechCrunch, "Microsoft's Satya Nadella says AI models need an 'emergency brake'" (October 10)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/10/microsofts-satya-nadella-says-ai-models-need-an-emergency-brake/); [Nadella's post on X](https://x.com/satyanadella/status/2108931348857827686) (the page returned a payment-required error when I tried to open it; the quotes are as TechCrunch reports them).*

### Atomic Machines Exits Stealth With $250 Million and a Switch for 800-Volt Data Centers

**What happened.** On October 7, Atomic Machines announced it is emerging after six years in stealth with $250 million. Its founder and chief executive, Jeff Holden, previously helped build Amazon Prime and was Uber's first chief product officer, according to The Next Web. Investors named include OneIM, Gigafund, XTX Ventures, KAS Venture Partners, Valor Equity Partners, Sozo Ventures and the Regents of the University of California. The company has not disclosed a valuation or how the money splits across rounds.

The company's core product is what it calls a "Matter Compiler," a system that builds micro-machines directly from code, without molds or fixed tooling. Its first product, the PrimeSwitch PS-150, is a relay, a switch that opens and closes a circuit, for data centers moving to 800-volt direct-current power. Per the company, as relayed by The Next Web, it opens in about 50 microseconds, roughly 1,000 times faster than a conventional contactor, carries 150 amps continuously and measures 9.5 by 3 by 3 millimeters. It is shipping to unnamed early-access customers and will be shown publicly at the Open Compute Project Global Summit in San Jose, October 12–15.

**Why it matters.** The company pitches the switch at 800-volt direct-current data center power, a shift it says is under way. I did not independently check that claim about the industry. The speed and size figures are Atomic Machines' own, and I found no independent test. The company also says the manufacturing method could someday make robots small enough for work inside the body and cooling built into chips. Those are aspirations, not products.

**Business implication.** The money here is going to a component supplier, not a model lab. Power equipment is a constraint on how fast data centers can be built, so an early-access product shown at the industry's main hardware conference this week is the first real test of whether the claims hold up with buyers.

*Sources: [Atomic Machines, Business Wire announcement (October 7)](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20261007902471/en/Atomic-Machines-Emerges-from-Stealth-with-%24250-Million-to-Launch-the-Matter-Compiler-an-AI-Native-Manufacturing-System-That-Builds-Micro-Machines-from-Code) (link as given by The Next Web; I read The Next Web's account, not the release itself); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/atomic-machines-250m-matter-compiler-micro-machines).*

### Apple Tells EU Regulators It Will Hire Huxe Staff and License Its Technology

**What happened.** Apple notified the European Commission on June 9 of an agreement to make employment offers to "certain employees of Huxe AI" and to receive a non-exclusive license to Huxe's intellectual property, according to TechCrunch, which cites the filing. MacRumors reported the filing on October 9. Huxe was founded by former members of Google's NotebookLM team, which made AI-generated podcast-style audio. It announced on May 21 that it was shutting down, and its service ended on May 28. The filing does not say who received offers, whether anyone accepted, what Apple paid, or what Apple will build. TechCrunch notes the deal does not involve an SEC filing; the notice is in the Commission's Digital Markets Act acquisitions database, which I did not search for the specific entry.

**Why it matters.** Large companies have used this structure, hiring a team and licensing its technology instead of buying the company, to get talent without a formal acquisition. Under the Digital Markets Act, the EU's rules for the largest tech platforms, Apple must tell the Commission about acquisitions, which is how the arrangement became public four months later. Whether Apple plans AI-generated audio for its Podcasts app is speculation from TechCrunch; Apple has said nothing.

**Business implication.** For founders of shut-down startups, a team-plus-license deal is a real exit route. For regulators, the question is whether these deals get the same scrutiny as acquisitions.

*Sources: [MacRumors (October 9)](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/10/09/apple-signs-hiring-deal-ai-podcast-startup-huxe/); [TechCrunch (October 10)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/10/apple-discloses-deal-to-hire-team-and-license-tech-from-personalized-podcast-startup-huxe/); [European Commission DMA acquisitions database](https://digital-markets-act-cases.ec.europa.eu/acquisitions) (the database, not the specific entry, which I did not open).*

## Model and Product Updates

**Liquid AI releases d1-3B, a model that returns decisions instead of text.** Liquid AI published d1-3B on Hugging Face, an open-weights model, meaning anyone can download it. Its model card describes a 3.12-billion-parameter "decision model" built on the company's LFM2.5-VL-3B. It takes text, structured data or images plus named questions and returns typed answers "in one forward pass with zero output tokens." It is not a chat model. The card reports a score of 48.57 on its Decision Index 0.2.1, which it calls the best under 10 billion parameters, ahead of a 35-billion-parameter model at 47.11 and just behind a 12-billion-parameter model at 50.02. It reports single-question latency of 8 milliseconds on an Nvidia RTX 4090 graphics card and 30 milliseconds on an Apple M5 Pro. These are Liquid AI's own results, on a benchmark the card ties to its own work; I found no independent evaluation. The card does not give an exact release date; other coverage says October 5 or 7 and I could not settle it. A search summary says a companion d1-omni-600M handles audio, and that commercial use above $10 million in annual revenue needs a separate license; I did not confirm either on the primary page. The design echoes this week's other non-text model, TypeSafe AI's Jev, covered yesterday: small, fast models that output probabilities or labels.

*Source: [Liquid AI, d1-3B model card on Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/d1-3B).*

## Regulation and Policy Watch

**UN human rights chief: "We can no longer afford to wait or rely on goodwill."** Volker Türk, the UN High Commissioner for Human Rights, told an AI and human rights summit in Oxford on October 5 that the world is running short of time to set safeguards. UN News's summary quotes him describing a "ruthless race" between companies and countries and saying "too few are walking the talk," a reference to a letter he sent three weeks earlier. He called for a ban on machines deciding who lives or dies, mandatory human rights due diligence in AI development, and updated liability, cybersecurity and data protection rules. UN News says a joint call for tighter control of frontier models is backed by 29 states and the European Union. It also describes a voluntary accord signed at the White House on September 29 by President Trump and the heads of six AI companies, with no penalties, which Trump called "morally binding." The same day, UN News notes, the New York City Council held a hearing on 10 AI bills, with testimony from Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and Meta.

**The White House pact.** TechCrunch, in an October 4 commentary, names it the "Joint Commitment on Frontier Responsibilities" and calls it non-binding; its article does not list specific obligations, and I could not find the signed text on a White House page. Treat the terms as unverified beyond "voluntary."

*Sources: [UN News, "'The clock on AI regulation is ticking', warns UN rights chief" (October 5)](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/10/1168529); [TechCrunch (October 4)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/04/can-super-intelligence-and-a-non-binding-safety-pact-solve-ais-image-problem/).*
