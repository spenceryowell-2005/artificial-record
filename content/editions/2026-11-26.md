## The Executive Read

The thread today is who bears the risk when AI companies change hands, change leaders or change course. S&P Global Ratings, according to TechCrunch's account of its statement, is considering lowering Nvidia's credit rating, citing weak executive oversight and dependence on Jensen Huang. That matters beyond Nvidia, because smaller data center builders borrow against its credit. Amazon announced it will buy the technology behind Decart's software for getting more speed out of AI chips, a bet that software can narrow Nvidia's lead. The price, about $7.5 billion, is Bloomberg's reporting, not Amazon's. On the consumer side, California says it leads a coalition of attorneys general asking a bankruptcy court to stop the sale of Character.AI's user chats, including data from more than 2 million minors' accounts. New York has signed a five-year ban on AI chatbot toys. OpenAI and Cisco released an open-source tool for patching Linux servers while a permanent fix is tested, and OpenAI published a medical-chemistry benchmark in which the top scores are its own models'. Most figures today come from the companies or from the officials who announced them. We say which. Nothing here is investment advice.

## Top AI Headlines

### S&P Weighs a Downgrade of Nvidia, Citing Governance and "Key Man" Risk

**What happened.** TechCrunch reported on Nov. 25 that S&P Global Ratings is considering lowering Nvidia's rating from AA to AA-, and expects to finish its review within 90 days. S&P's reasoning, as TechCrunch relays it, is that the ouster and reinstatement of Huang as chief executive raised concerns about "weak executive oversight and lack of succession planning" and "key man risk." Key man risk means a company leans heavily on one person. TechCrunch says Nvidia is still expected to keep an investment-grade rating, and that its article includes no comment from Nvidia. We could not open S&P's statement, which returned an access error, so the wording comes from TechCrunch. Separately, TechCrunch reported, citing Wired, that at his first all-hands meeting since returning, Huang said he had violated Nvidia's code of conduct and that no one is exempt from it. Wired also reported, per TechCrunch, that Huang said breaking the code does not automatically mean dismissal. We have not read Wired's article.

**Why it matters.** A credit rating sets how cheaply a company can borrow. TechCrunch says Nvidia backstops neoclouds, the smaller companies that rent out GPUs, and other infrastructure builders that lack strong credit alone. In August, per Bloomberg as relayed by TechCrunch, Nvidia announced plans to arrange $500 billion in AI infrastructure financing with six institutions, including Goldman Sachs, Apollo and Blackstone. Under the original terms Nvidia would guarantee 25% of the loans. Bloomberg reported on Nov. 12 that lenders had been pressing Nvidia to guarantee more, even before Huang's exit. TechCrunch's reading is that a downgrade would likely push lenders to ask for larger guarantees. That is its inference, not S&P's finding.

**Business implication.** Even a one-notch cut leaves Nvidia highly rated. The practical question is the guarantee terms, because they decide how much debt-funded data center building gets done. Customers financing capacity against Nvidia's backing should watch whether lenders reprice. Nvidia's own disclosures, including its [Nov. 23 SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000090/nov23pressrelease.htm), describe the board's governance changes: a new committee on export controls and a search for a chief compliance officer.

*Sources: [TechCrunch on the S&P review, Nov. 25](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/sp-considers-nvidia-credit-downgrade-over-governance-and-key-man-risks/); [TechCrunch on the all-hands, Nov. 25 (citing Wired)](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/jensen-huang-broke-nvidias-code-of-conduct-and-his-employees-may-not-forget-it/); [Nvidia press release, Form 8-K (SEC), Nov. 23](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000090/nov23pressrelease.htm).*

### Amazon Agrees to Buy Decart's Chip-Optimization Technology

**What happened.** On Nov. 23, Amazon announced it signed an agreement to acquire the proprietary technology behind the Decart Optimization Stack, known as DOS. Decart's co-founders Dean Leitersdorf, Orian Leitersdorf and Moshe Shalev, with their researchers and engineers, will join Amazon. Amazon says it has worked with Decart since 2025 on performance on its Trainium chips. It says the aim is faster inference, meaning running a trained model to answer requests, and better efficiency on its custom silicon, including Trainium and Graviton. The deal is subject to customary closing conditions, including regulatory clearance. Amazon did not disclose a price. Bloomberg reported the price at about $7.5 billion, citing unnamed sources; we link its page but could not read it. TechCrunch adds that Anthropic reportedly tried to buy Decart for a similar amount in September and walked away, and that Nvidia had invested in Decart's $300 million round at a $4 billion valuation. Both of those are TechCrunch's reporting, not confirmed by the companies in anything we saw. TechCrunch also says Amazon is not acquiring Decart's world-model work, which is software that simulates environments.

**Why it matters.** Most AI software is tuned for Nvidia's chips and its CUDA programming layer, which keeps customers on Nvidia hardware. Optimization software that makes other chips run models well is one way to loosen that. Amazon executive Peter DeSantis said in the release: "Every leap forward in AI starts with getting more out of every chip." That is a statement of intent, and Amazon published no performance numbers.

**Business implication.** Per TechCrunch, Amazon wants more performance from Trainium and could eventually offer Trainium-based capacity to more customers. Whether DOS closes the gap with Nvidia is unproven. Watch for regulatory review and for any published benchmarks on Trainium.

*Sources: [Amazon announcement, Nov. 23](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/amazon-decart-ai-optimization); [Bloomberg on the reported price, Nov. 23](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-23/amazon-is-said-to-pay-7-5-billion-for-ai-startup-decart-s-tech); [TechCrunch, Nov. 24](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/amazons-decart-acquisition-is-a-bet-on-using-software-to-narrow-the-gap-with-nvidia-in-the-ai-chip-race/).*

### State Attorneys General Move to Block the Sale of Character.AI Users' Chats

**What happened.** California Attorney General Rob Bonta announced on Nov. 24 that he and a coalition of attorneys general asked the bankruptcy court to reject the proposed sale of nearly all of Character.AI's assets. The coalition also asked the court to reject Smirks and Pangs Acquisition LLC as lead bidder and to deny a proposed $350,000 break-up fee, a payment to a bidder if a deal falls through. At a minimum, it asks that children's chats and other sensitive data be excluded and that users be protected. Bonta's release gives the data at more than 21 million U.S. accounts, including more than 2.1 million registered to minors, and says the auction is set for Dec. 1. The release counts the coalition as 42 attorneys general in one place and 45 in a Bonta quote; TechCrunch says the list includes the District of Columbia. "Bankruptcy should not be used to sidestep these consumer protections," Bonta said. The release does not name the court or case number. TechCrunch reports Character.AI filed for Chapter 11 in October and that the company had told users their personal information would not be sold, per the attorneys general. It reports the only disclosed bidder is a Delaware company formed on Nov. 13.

**Why it matters.** The dispute is over whether a privacy promise survives a bankruptcy. TechCrunch says the attorneys general argue a buyer would not be bound by Character.AI's privacy policy and could resell the data. They also argue the sale could trigger violations of chatbot safety laws taking effect Jan. 1, 2027 in several states, including California, Colorado, New York and Connecticut. These are the attorneys general's arguments; the court has not ruled in anything we found.

**Business implication.** Any AI startup holding intimate user conversations is holding an asset a bankruptcy trustee may try to sell. Expect the outcome to shape how companies write privacy terms for chatbot data. TechCrunch also reports that a similar coalition opposes a proposed $20 million deal giving Disney access to Character.AI's intellectual property, which does not include user chats. That deal does not yet have final court approval.

*Sources: [California Attorney General press release, Nov. 24](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-opposes-asset-sale-characterai-amid-child-safety-concerns); [TechCrunch, Nov. 25](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/state-ags-fight-character-ais-plan-to-auction-off-users-private-chats/).*

### New York Signs a Five-Year Ban on AI Chatbot Toys

**What happened.** Gov. Kathy Hochul signed a bill on Nov. 24, according to her office's release, that bans manufacturing, distributing and selling toys with embedded AI chatbots for five years. The bill is S.9408A/A.11144B. It takes effect 90 days after signing, which is around late February 2027. The release says the attorney general enforces it, with civil penalties of up to $15,000 per day for each violation. TechCrunch says New York is the second state after California to ban AI toys, and that Amazon said it will restrict sales of AI-enabled toys in prohibited jurisdictions, including by third-party sellers, without giving details. We did not read the bill text.

**Why it matters.** The ban covers toys such as talking stuffed animals and internet-connected robots, and it lands at the start of the holiday shopping season. TechCrunch links a November Common Sense Media study that says popular AI toys fail safety checks. We did not open that study, and we note it is an advocacy group's finding.

**Business implication.** Toy makers selling chatbot-enabled products into New York have about three months to pull them. Retailers, especially marketplaces with third-party sellers, take on the burden of screening listings. Penalties scale with time, so a slow takedown is expensive.

*Sources: [New York Governor's office release, Nov. 24](https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/consumers-begin-their-holiday-shopping-season-governor-hochul-signs-five-year-ban-ai-chatbot); [TechCrunch, Nov. 25](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/want-to-buy-your-kid-an-ai-toy-this-christmas-not-in-new-york/).*

### OpenAI and Cisco Release Shirudo, an Open-Source Stopgap for Unpatched Linux Servers

**What happened.** On Nov. 23, OpenAI announced Shirudo, Japanese for "shield," built with Cisco. It is an open-source runtime that lets operators protect running Linux systems while a permanent patch is tested and rolled out. It uses eBPF, a Linux feature that runs small checked programs inside the operating system to watch activity and apply narrow protections. Per OpenAI, shields can block a vulnerable code path without a restart, and teams can watch a proposed shield's effect before enforcing it. Shields are cryptographically signed and verified before loading, and a hardware chip called a TPM keeps a verifiable record of shields being loaded or removed. OpenAI's one test: a shield generated with its Codex coding agent added an input check to Java's Base64 decoder, was attached to a running Elasticsearch process, and rejected a Java4Shell exploit attempt with no restart.

**Why it matters.** Patching can take days or weeks, and an exploit and a legitimate request can use the same system operation, so blocking the operation outright can take a service down. Shirudo aims at that gap. OpenAI's page is open about limits: it is a temporary safeguard, not a replacement for patching, and security teams must approve each shield. The agent-driven shield creation is described as a design goal, not a shipped feature. The page gives no false-positive rates, performance overhead, or supported kernel versions. The evidence is a single controlled test from OpenAI.

**Business implication.** Source code is on GitHub under a BSD 2-Clause license, with a virtual-machine quickstart, and a technical webinar is set for Dec. 15. Security teams that run large Linux fleets can try it. Whether it holds up outside OpenAI's own demo is the open question.

*Sources: [OpenAI, "OpenAI and Cisco introduce Shirudo," Nov. 23](https://openai.com/index/introducing-shirudo/).*

## Model and Product Updates

**OpenAI's MedChemBench.** On Nov. 20, OpenAI published MedChemBench, a benchmark of 99 problems drawn from 117 published papers and patents, covering 761 molecular structures. Medicinal chemistry is the work of designing and choosing drug candidates, where improving one property, such as potency, can worsen another, such as how fast the body breaks the molecule down. Tasks are graded on rebuilding molecular structures, scientific explanation, ranking and selecting compounds, and predicting numerical properties. At OpenAI's highest reasoning setting it reports overall scores of 84.5% for GPT-6.2 Astra, 83.3% for GPT-6 Astra, 82.0% for GPT-6.1 Sol, 75.1% for GPT-6.1 Terra and 58.2% for GPT-5.6 Sol. These are OpenAI testing its own models on a benchmark it built, and the page lists no other labs' models. OpenAI states the limits: the results do not show whether models can discover drugs, models may have seen the source papers in training, and each answer was a single attempt without tools.

*Sources: [OpenAI, MedChemBench, Nov. 20](https://openai.com/index/introducing-medchembench/).*

**Google adds Wallet and Drive to AI Mode.** Google announced on Nov. 23 that Personal Intelligence in Search's AI Mode, which uses your own data for answers, now covers Google Wallet, with Drive and the Gemini app following "in the coming weeks." It already connected to Gmail, Calendar and Photos. Google says Wallet access can help map spending and optimize credit card rewards, and that AI Mode "can only read" Wallet information and cannot move or manage money. Connections are opt-in. The post does not list countries or subscription tiers. A "Personalization" icon showing what context was used is promised "soon."

*Sources: [Google, Search blog, Nov. 23](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/google-search-personal-intelligence-apps/).*

**Walmart opens Black Friday shopping inside Meta's Muse.** Walmart said on Nov. 24 that customers can shop its Black Friday deals directly through Muse, Meta's personal AI agent, finding items, building a basket and completing the purchase in the conversation using a card saved in the Walmart account. It runs on Walmart's Agent Gateway. Through Agent Profiles, users can set spending limits, approve purchases and review what the agent bought. Walmart says Muse does not get a customer's Walmart shopping history. The page does not specify regions. TechCrunch notes the contrast with Amazon, which has blocked third-party agents from buying on its site; yesterday's edition covered reported talks between Amazon and Meta on that.

*Sources: [Walmart, Nov. 24](https://corporate.walmart.com/news/2026/11/black-friday-deals-with-meta-muse); [TechCrunch, Nov. 24](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/walmart-teams-up-with-meta-to-bring-black-friday-deals-to-ai-agent-muse/).*

**ChatGPT merges Chat and Work.** TechCrunch reports that OpenAI has begun rolling out a merged Chat and Work experience to a small group of Pro subscribers, announced by product lead Thibault Sottiaux in an X post. The two share one usage meter. Conversations on the Instant model stay unlimited. An OpenAI spokesperson said most users should not see more frequent resets but "a small fraction of its heaviest users may hit their limits sooner." Per TechCrunch's reading of OpenAI's support page, broader rollout of shared limits is planned for December. Some subscribers have complained on X, OpenAI's forum and Reddit. We did not find OpenAI's own post on the change, so this rests on TechCrunch.

*Sources: [TechCrunch, Nov. 25](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/openai-says-heavy-chatgpt-users-may-hit-limits-sooner-as-it-merges-chat-and-work/).*

## Regulation and Policy Watch

**Amodei answers rivals on human control.** In a long post on X on Nov. 25, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei called "absurd" the claim that Anthropic wants people to surrender human free will to AI, TechCrunch reports. He wrote: "We should never sacrifice legitimacy or self-determination at the altar of efficiency or security." He said he opposes governance by AI and favors democratic oversight, and accused competitors of "vibes-based commercial and political positioning." TechCrunch says the accusation comes from a broader group of rivals, citing remarks by OpenAI's Sam Altman on Nov. 18 and an essay by Meta's Alexandr Wang. TechCrunch also reports Anthropic has said Claude wrote over 80% of the code merged into its codebase, and that a METR assessment in November found Anthropic's models improving at about twice their historical rate. We did not read Amodei's post, the METR assessment or Wang's essay. This is a policy argument among company executives, not a regulatory action.

*Sources: [TechCrunch, Nov. 25](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/anthropics-dario-amodei-calls-accusations-of-surrendering-human-control-to-ai-absurd/).*

**Tidal bans fully AI-generated tracks.** This is platform policy, not law. Per TechCrunch, Tidal will ban and remove fully AI-generated tracks starting Dec. 9, while AI-assisted songs remain allowed. Tidal's figures, as TechCrunch reports them, are that more than 43% of tracks uploaded in the past 90 days were AI-generated, that about 27 million tracks (6.3% of its catalog) are fully AI-generated, and that 96% of those have no streams. It expects more than 29 million deletions by year-end. Those are the company's numbers. TechCrunch says Bandcamp banned AI music outright, Deezer is adding a filter and Spotify and YouTube Music are labeling it. Tidal's announcement is on X; we link TechCrunch's report.

*Sources: [TechCrunch, Nov. 24](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/tidal-to-ban-and-remove-millions-of-ai-generated-tracks-as-ai-slop-inundates-music-streaming/).*

## Emerging Startup Radar

**Supertake, $20 million seed.** TechCrunch reports the round was led by Adjacent, with Coinbase Ventures and Union Square Ventures participating, and angels including Nat Friedman and Guillermo Rauch. It follows a $2 million pre-seed. Valuation was not disclosed. Users describe a market view in plain language, the AI turns it into a stock portfolio, and ideas go on a leaderboard where others can invest alongside. Portfolios are currently stock-only, via brokerage partners Coinbase and Robinhood. Supertake says the AI cannot deposit or withdraw funds without explicit consent and cannot access users' brokerage credentials, and it lists trade-size limits and anomaly monitoring. Those safeguards are the company's description. The product is in beta behind a waitlist, and TechCrunch's article does not discuss regulatory status. Founders say they use the product with their own money; those claims are unverified. Michael Mignano, who incubated the company at USV, is executive chairman. Lincoln Murr is CEO and John Peterson CTO.

*Sources: [TechCrunch, Nov. 23](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/23/supertake-raises-20m-to-let-ai-agents-turn-your-market-predictions-into-stock-portfolios/).*

## AI Infrastructure and Market Signals

**Power: one investor's case for solar and batteries.** TechCrunch interviewed Brook Porter of G2 Venture Partners, which estimates data center investment will exceed $4 trillion through the end of the decade, per an analysis the firm published. Porter says global natural gas capacity grows by only about 60 to 70 gigawatts a year, while solar is "already at well over a terawatt" a year of capacity. A gigawatt is roughly the output of one large nuclear reactor. He sees small on-grid data centers with their own generation, and larger off-grid sites run on solar plus long-duration storage, naming Form Energy and Antora Energy, the latter a portfolio company of his firm. This is a venture investor with a stake in the answer, so read it as a view, not a forecast. We did not open G2VP's analysis.

*Sources: [TechCrunch, Nov. 24](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/24/why-a-crusoe-investor-is-betting-on-solar-and-batteries-to-power-future-ai-data-centers/).*
