## The Executive Read

Most of this week's AI news has been about people and paperwork: a chief executive's resignation, an IPO prospectus, a lawsuit. Thursday evening brought something plainer, which was two companies that sell the physical parts of AI saying that orders are still arriving. Applied Materials, which makes the machines that build chips, reported record revenue of $10.41 billion for its fiscal fourth quarter, up 53% from a year earlier. Cisco, which sells the networking gear inside data centers, reported revenue up 24% and raised its full-year forecast, with its own figure for hyperscaler AI infrastructure revenue now at $9 billion. Both numbers are the companies' own, in their own releases. The throughline is that the spending is moving down the supply chain, from model labs to equipment, cables and power. The Financial Times reported on Sunday that Anthropic has agreed to pay $3.4 billion for gas-fired generators; neither Anthropic nor the supplier commented to the FT, and we have not seen a company confirmation. And Mercor, which pays experts to train and test AI models, said on Tuesday it raised $750 million at a $20 billion valuation, a round this publication declined to report yesterday because we could not then find a primary source. We have one now. Nvidia reports its first results since Jensen Huang resigned on Tuesday.

## Top AI Headlines

### Applied Materials Reports Record $10.41 Billion Quarter and Guides Higher

**What happened.** Applied Materials released its fiscal fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 results on November 12, in an SEC filing. Quarterly revenue was a record $10.41 billion, up 53%. Adjusted earnings per share (profit per share, excluding certain accounting items) were a record $4.16, up 92%; the figure under standard accounting rules was $3.40. Gross margin was 50.4% on that standard basis and 50.6% adjusted. For the full year, revenue was $34.45 billion, up 21%, and adjusted earnings were $12.89 a share, up 37%. The company guided to revenue of $11.1 billion, plus or minus $500 million, and adjusted earnings of $4.35, plus or minus 20 cents, for its first quarter of fiscal 2027.

The breakdown matters. Semiconductor Systems, the unit that sells the chipmaking machines, had revenue of $7.996 billion in the quarter, against $4.879 billion a year earlier, an increase of about 64% by our arithmetic. Foundry and logic customers, who make processors, were 58% of that unit's quarter; DRAM memory was 33% and flash 9%. China was 25% of total revenue, or $2.642 billion, down from 29% a year earlier. For the full year China was 27%, down from 30%. Applied Materials said it is on track to grow Semiconductor Systems by more than 40% in calendar 2026.

**Why it matters.** CEO Gary Dickerson said in the release that "AI continues to fuel strong incremental demand for semiconductors." That is a company statement, but the structure of the numbers supports the direction of it. Chipmakers buy these machines before they sell chips, so equipment revenue is an early signal of what chip factories expect to build. Memory being a third of the unit's quarter suggests memory makers are buying too, not only the foundries. The falling China share is also worth noting. Applied did not say why in the release, and we are not offering a cause.

**Business implication.** The company returned cash on a large scale: $2.0 billion in share repurchases and $421 million in dividends in the quarter, which the company's finance chief described as the largest quarterly buyback in more than eight years. This is informational, not a view on the stock. Separately, the release said Applied unveiled its EPIC Center, a shared research facility in Silicon Valley, and added four partners: Intel as founding partner, KIOXIA, Besi and UCLA Samueli, for a total of 15 announced engagements.

*Sources: [Applied Materials, fourth-quarter and fiscal 2026 results, Exhibit 99.1 to Form 8-K (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000006951/000162828026073360/exhibit991q42026earningsre.htm). All figures are the company's own; we did not compare them with analyst estimates from a primary source.*

### Cisco Raises Its Fiscal 2027 Outlook and Doubles Its Hyperscaler AI Revenue Target

**What happened.** Cisco reported results for its fiscal first quarter, which ended October 24, after the market closed on November 12. Revenue was $18.5 billion, up 24%. Adjusted earnings per share were $1.40, up 40%, and $1.14 on the standard basis, up 58%. Cisco said revenue, adjusted operating margin and adjusted earnings per share each came in above the high end of its own guidance. Chair and CEO Chuck Robbins said the quarter was the strongest for revenue and adjusted earnings growth in more than 15 years. That is the company's characterization.

Networking was the engine. Networking revenue was $11.132 billion, up 43%. Security was $2.12 billion, up 7%. Collaboration was $1.136 billion, up 8%, and Observability was $279 million, up 2%. Product revenue rose 32% while services rose 1%. Americas revenue rose 28%, EMEA 18% and Asia-Pacific, Japan and China 21%. Cisco raised its full-year fiscal 2027 revenue forecast to $74.8–75.8 billion and its adjusted earnings forecast to $5.47–5.53 a share. In August it had guided to $72.2–73.4 billion and $5.05–5.11, per Cisco's August guidance. For the second quarter it guided to revenue of $18.0–18.2 billion and adjusted earnings of $1.34–1.36.

**Why it matters.** The release raises Cisco's expected fiscal 2027 revenue from hyperscaler AI infrastructure to $9 billion, which it says is more than double the fiscal 2026 total. Hyperscalers are the very large cloud companies, such as Amazon, Microsoft and Google. The release describes this as a revenue expectation and does not disclose an orders figure, so we cannot say how much of it is already contracted. Robbins said, "We are in the very early stages of a multi-year networking supercycle." Treat that as the sales pitch it is. The data underneath it is more useful. Remaining performance obligations, a measure of contracted revenue not yet recognized, were $46.2 billion, up 8%. That grew far more slowly than revenue; the release does not say why.

**Business implication.** Margins fell even as sales rose. Adjusted gross margin was 65.6%, down from 68.1% a year earlier, with product gross margin at 63.7% against 67.2%. The release does not give a reason.  Adjusted operating margin still reached a record 37.8%, because operating expenses fell. Cisco returned $3.7 billion to shareholders, bought back about 18 million shares at an average of $112.38, and closed its acquisition of WideField Security, a private security company. This is informational only.

*Sources: [Cisco, "Cisco Reports First Quarter Earnings" (November 12)](https://newsroom.cisco.com/c/r/newsroom/en/us/a/y2026/m11/cisco-reports-first-quarter-earnings.html); [Cisco investor relations, earnings-call scheduling notice](https://investor.cisco.com/news/news-details/2026/Cisco-Schedules-Conference-Call-for-Q1-Fiscal-Year-2027-Financial-Results/default.aspx).*

### Anthropic Reportedly Agreed to Pay $3.4 Billion for Gas Generators, According to the Financial Times

**What happened.** The Financial Times reported on Sunday, November 8, that Anthropic agreed in late September to pay about $3.4 billion for commitments covering 1.7 gigawatts of heavy-duty, gas-fired generators. A gigawatt is roughly the output of one large nuclear reactor, so this is about one and a half to two reactors' worth of equipment, though generators rarely run at full output around the clock. According to the FT's report as relayed by The Next Web and Fortune, the seller was OnePWR Solutions, a power supplier in San Antonio, Texas. OnePWR had ordered the generators from Caterpillar about two years earlier but needed funding to complete the purchase. Anthropic had first planned to buy power from OnePWR, then took over the equipment orders and the engineering and construction work instead. It did not buy OnePWR, and none of OnePWR's staff moved to Anthropic. The FT called it the biggest known single purchase of power equipment by an AI company.

The FT's account rests on one unnamed person with knowledge of the deal. Both companies declined to comment to the FT, and Fortune said neither responded to its requests. The FT report does not say where the generators will be used. Fortune says the generators are expected to become available within two years. We were not able to open the FT's own article, so we link the two outlets that relayed it; the reporting is the FT's.

**Why it matters.** The deal fits what Anthropic has said in a document we can check. According to Fortune's reading of Anthropic's amended S-1, the registration filed with the SEC for its planned public offering, the company has about $900.9 billion in total committed computing and infrastructure spending, with about $70.7 billion due in the 12 months after September 30. The Next Web's account of the same filing gives about $895 billion, so the two differ slightly, and we have not read the amended filing ourselves. Fortune also quotes the S-1 as warning about "increased competition among users for the same natural gas supply and pipeline capacity" in some areas, and says the filing warns that long-lead power equipment may have to be bought at above-market prices.

**Business implication.** If the report is accurate, an AI lab has moved from renting electricity to owning the machines that make it, which shortens the wait for power but adds fuel-price risk. Anthropic's roadshow for its IPO, announced Monday, is offering 825 million Class A shares at an expected $70 to $78 each, and the registration statement is not yet effective. Investors reading the prospectus are being asked to take a view on these commitments. Pricing is reported, not confirmed by the company, for around November 18, with trading the following day; Anthropic's announcement gives no date.

*Sources: Financial Times reporting (Nov. 8), relayed by [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/11/10/anthropic-3-4-billion-deal-gas-data-centers-ai-ipo) and [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/anthropic-3-4bn-gas-generators-onepwr-ft); [Anthropic, roadshow announcement (Nov. 10)](https://www.anthropic.com/news/roadshow-launch). The FT article itself was not retrievable by our tools; the deal has not been confirmed by Anthropic or OnePWR.*

### Mercor Says It Raised $750 Million at a $20 Billion Valuation

**What happened.** Mercor announced a $750 million Series D on November 10 at a $20 billion valuation. The company's own post lists the investors: Benchmark, Felicis, Fundomo, funds managed by affiliates of Apollo, G Squared, General Catalyst, Glade Brook Capital, Nvidia, Salesforce, Sapphire Ventures, StepStone Group, XTX Ventures and Zoom. The post does not name a lead, does not give a revenue figure, and does not say how much of the $750 million is an employee tender, a sale of existing shares by staff rather than new money for the company. According to The Next Web, Mercor's valuation was $10 billion when it raised $350 million in October 2025.

**A correction.** Our November 12 edition said a $750 million Mercor round at $20 billion "does not match what we found" and withheld it. That was the right call on the evidence we had that day, which was reports of talks. Mercor's own November 10 post confirms the round, so the withheld claim is now supported. We should have found the company post; we did not.

**Why it matters.** Mercor's business is paying professionals to produce the examples and tests that AI models learn from. The Next Web describes a network of more than five million experts, with frontier labs as the main buyers so far and the new money meant to sell similar services to other companies. The company's only financial disclosure in the post is a ratio: "We already spend 3x as much on inference as on employee salaries." Inference is the cost of running a model to produce answers. Another line in the post reads, "A growing number of companies are realizing that owning their intelligence is essential." Both are company statements.

**Business implication.** Nvidia and Salesforce are on the investor list, and both also sell to the model builders Mercor serves. The Next Web notes that an April security breach led Meta to freeze its AI data work with Mercor, and that Mercor's funding statement does not mention it. We have not verified the breach independently. The valuation doubled in a year with no revenue disclosed.

*Sources: [Mercor, Series D announcement (Nov. 10)](https://www.mercor.com/blog/series-d/); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/mercor-750m-series-d-20bn-valuation). Mercor's statement is the sole primary source; the breach detail is The Next Web's.*

## Regulation and Policy Watch

**Illinois governor sets out a federal AI agenda.** According to Capitol News Illinois, in a story republished by WIFR, Governor JB Pritzker released a suggested 200-point national agenda for the incoming Democratic Congress on Thursday. It calls for a federal framework covering privacy, AI safety and data centers. Its AI items include federal requirements for risk assessment, testing and monitoring, and a requirement that data centers be energy- and water-efficient. Pritzker said federal action should build on what states have done rather than override it. That puts him against the preemption approach, in which federal law would displace state AI rules. The report says Illinois became the third state, after New York and California, to pass frontier AI regulation in July. Its Senate Bill 315 covers models from developers with more than $500 million in annual revenue and requires an annual third-party audit, according to the report. The report adds that there is no comprehensive federal framework today, that it is unclear whether President Trump would sign one, and that Trump's September executive order and the White House accord on superintelligence are voluntary and nonbinding. An industry trade group quoted, the Computer and Communications Industry Association, expects "a very active 2027 around AI." The December 2025 executive order that started the federal push against state AI laws is on the [White House site](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/12/eliminating-state-law-obstruction-of-national-artificial-intelligence-policy/). *Sources: [Capitol News Illinois via WIFR/Rockford News First](https://rockfordnewsfirst.com/2026/11/12/with-new-democratic-congress-incoming-pritzker-calls-federal-ai-regulations); [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/12/eliminating-state-law-obstruction-of-national-artificial-intelligence-policy/).*

**Nvidia's succession is now a governance question, in the company's own words.** Nvidia's November 12 filing says the board is searching for a permanent chief executive and will consider internal and external candidates. Colette Kress, who has been chief financial officer since September 2013, is interim CEO while keeping that job, and new board chair Harvey C. Jones said the board "has full confidence in Colette and the leadership team." The filing gives no reason for Huang's resignation. Earlier reporting about a congressional request for Huang to brief House committees appeared in our November 12 edition; we found no confirmed update today. *Source: [Nvidia Form 8-K (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000082/nvidiaannouncesceotransiti.htm).*

## Emerging Startup Radar

**Path Robotics, $300 million Series E.** The Ohio company, which makes AI-guided welding robots, raised $300 million at a $2.45 billion valuation on November 10, according to The Next Web. Georgian led, with D. E. Shaw Ventures and Naver Ventures joining, and existing backers Addition, Drive Capital, IronArc Ventures, Tiger Global and Yamaha Motor Ventures taking part. The company says annual recurring revenue grew 17-fold over 18 months, and says total funding is now above $610 million. Customers named are HII, Lockheed Martin and Saronic. Path says it signed a $600 million performance-based production agreement with HII for U.S. Navy shipbuilding earlier this year. Its Rove product puts its welding software on a Boston Dynamics quadruped robot; the company says it has 50 preorders. CEO Andy Lonsberry said, "None of our customers have ever fired a welder because of a task robot." That is a claim by the company's chief executive. We did not find Path's own release, so these figures rest on a trade report of the company's statements. *Source: [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/path-robotics-300m-series-e-2-45bn-valuation).*

**Rox, $100 million Series B.** Fortune reported on November 11 that Rox, which sells AI sales software, raised $100 million at a $1.3 billion valuation. General Catalyst and March Capital co-led; Sequoia and GV returned and Prosperity7 joined. Rox did not disclose revenue and said only that annual recurring revenue has grown more than tenfold since its first year after launch. Its agents research customer accounts, suggest which prospects to prioritize and draft outreach. Named customers include Ramp, New Relic, MongoDB and Groq. *Source: [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/11/11/rox-series-b-100-million-funding-take-on-salesforce).*

**Nebuly, $21 million Series A.** The Turin-based company, which helps businesses measure the financial return on their AI agents, announced the round on November 12, according to The Next Web. Planven led, with Exor Ventures, Tenacity Capital, TGVG Capital, Fastweb+Vodafone and CDP Venture Capital's funds taking part. Customers named include Oura, Iveco Group, CNH and Zeiss, with contracts it describes as multiple six-figure sums. The pitch is that companies are spending on agents without a way to show what the agents earn or save. *Source: [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/nebuly-21m-series-a-enterprise-ai-roi).*

## Forward-Looking Angle

The useful question this week is where AI spending lands once it leaves the labs. Today's evidence points to three places: factory equipment, network gear and power plants. The reported Anthropic generators are expected within about two years, per Fortune. A lab that buys all three is committing to a demand forecast several years long, and its IPO documents say so in the risk section. We do not know how those forecasts will turn out; it is worth reading the next set of earnings for the first sign that lead times are shortening, which would mean the supply constraint is easing. That is our reading, not a statement from any company.
