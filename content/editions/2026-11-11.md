## The Executive Read

On November 10, three of the largest AI companies each tried to move closer to the place where work actually happens. Google made Gemini 4 Carbon, its strongest model by its own description, available to paying customers at an introductory $2 per million input tokens. Anthropic said it was adding spreadsheets, whiteboards and partner apps to Claude, a day after it set an expected IPO price range of $70 to $78 a share. And OpenAI, according to a trade report we could read, opened a beta that lets businesses run AI sales agents inside ChatGPT, with a paid placement option. A token is a small chunk of text, roughly three-quarters of a word, and it is the unit all of these products are billed in. The benchmark scores for Carbon are Google's and its partners' own; we found no independent results. The same day produced a counterweight. Notion published a timeline saying a third-party AI vendor, Fireworks AI, exposed chat transcripts from its Insights feature, and Australia's prime minister said his government will create an AI safety regulator, according to AFP. The thread is that the products are now agents with real access, and the suppliers behind them are now a security question as well as a pricing question. Fortune reports that the Nasdaq fell 5.5% last week. Nothing in Anthropic's offering is complete, and none of this is investment advice.

## Top AI Headlines

### Google Releases Gemini 4 Carbon, Priced at $2 and $10 Per Million Tokens Until November 30

**What happened.** Google announced on November 10 that Gemini 4 Carbon, which it calls its strongest model, is available through the API in Google AI Studio and Gemini for business to paying customers. Subscribers to its Google AI Ultra plan get it in the Antigravity coding tool, the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search. Introductory pricing is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, the same as Gemini 4 Argon, the model Google positions for everyday work. After November 30, Google says, the public price rises to $4 and $20. Cybersecurity defenders can apply for stronger cyber capabilities through a program called Fairwind, and scientists can apply for six months of free access through Antigravity's top usage tier. Google also previewed a `/swarm` feature in Antigravity that coordinates several agents from one prompt.

Google reports that Carbon ranks first on CursorBench 4.0, Perplexity's WANDR and Zapier's AutomationBench at more than twice the cost-efficiency of the next-best external model. It reports second place on a multimodal agent benchmark called MKA at more than six times lower average cost per task than the top model. In its own Project Fortify security test, it says, Carbon reached 100% recall on scanning and 95% precision on triage. These are Google's and its partners' figures, and some come from pre-release evaluations.

**Why it matters.** The safety claims are specific. Google says Carbon blocked 99% of attack paths from an adaptive red-teaming agent, and that the per-attempt breach rate fell to 0.8% from 3.1% on Argon. It says outside testers at Trajectory Labs found no new universal jailbreaks, meaning single prompts that defeat a model's rules, across 132 hours and more than 13,000 requests. Google also says plainly that prompt injection, where hidden instructions in a document or web page hijack an agent, "remains an area for continued improvement" and that some sophisticated attacks can still succeed. Google says Carbon uses more tokens per task than Argon, so cost per task matters more than the price per token.

**Business implication.** The price doubles in 19 days. Teams planning to benchmark Carbon should do it before the end of the month and budget at the higher rate. Compare cost per finished task, not per token, because Google itself says Carbon thinks longer. Defenders who want the stronger cyber features have to apply to Fairwind.

*Sources: [Google, "Gemini 4 Carbon is now available" (November 10)](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-carbon-now-available/). All benchmark and security figures are Google's or its partners'; we found no independent results today.*

### Anthropic Adds Sheets, Whiteboards and Partner Apps to Claude

**What happened.** On November 10 Anthropic announced three new kinds of shared work files in Claude: Claude Sheets, a spreadsheet that Claude and a team build together, with formulas and multiple tabs; Claude Whiteboards, a shared board Claude can summarize; and Claude Data, which replaces what Anthropic called Dashboards and connects to tools such as Snowflake and Salesforce to produce dashboards, data stories or narrated short videos. A fourth, Claude Meetings, was announced as coming soon; Anthropic says it will join calls, take notes and update documents. Anthropic also expanded "interactive connectors," which let partner apps such as Adobe and Atlassian run inside Claude. Salesforce and Workday plugins are in beta, and a NetSuite plugin is included. Anthropic says its marketplace lists more than 9,000 connectors and plugins.

For Enterprise customers, the company lists admin controls over products and connectors, spend limits, audit logs and a Compliance API that works with more than 100 security tools. It says each cloud session on Enterprise runs in its own isolated virtual machine. Its page does not say which plans get Sheets, Whiteboards or Claude Data. As a customer example, it says Advantage Solutions cut the assembly of a financial forecast from about 10 hours to under 30 minutes; that is Anthropic's account of a customer's result.

**Why it matters.** This puts Claude in the territory of Microsoft Office and Google Workspace, and The Register framed it as a step into Office territory. The Register's account matches Anthropic's on the product list and gives no pricing. The timing matters too. The launch came a day after Anthropic set its IPO price range, and a company with an offering pending will be judged on whether customers adopt products like these.

**Business implication.** If your team already pays for Claude, ask which plan includes Sheets and Data, because the company's page does not say. For companies running Microsoft or Google suites, the near-term question is whether an AI-first spreadsheet is good enough to take real work away from them. Test that on one forecasting task before drawing conclusions.

*Sources: [Anthropic, "More ways to work with Claude: Sheets, Whiteboards and interactive connectors" (November 10)](https://claude.com/resources/articles/more-ways-to-work-with-claude-sheets-whiteboards-and-interactive-connectors); [The Register (November 10)](https://www.theregister.com/software/2026/11/10/anthropic-tiptoes-into-ms-office-territory/5308988).*

### Anthropic's IPO Range Implies Up to About $1.4 Trillion, According to Fortune, as AI Stocks Slide

**What happened.** Anthropic's own roadshow announcement, which we covered yesterday, says it expects to price 825 million Class A shares at $70 to $78 and has applied to list on Nasdaq as "ANT." We re-read the page today. It states no valuation, and it has not changed since November 10. Fortune reports that, on a fully diluted basis, the range would value Anthropic at up to $1.4 trillion, well below the roughly $2 trillion some outlets had reported weeks ago, and that the offering could raise up to $64.4 billion at the top of the range. Fortune does not attribute the valuation to Anthropic, and fully diluted counts every share that could exist, including options, so we treat the $1.4 trillion as Fortune's calculation. The same article reports that the Nasdaq fell 5.5% last week and says Nvidia shares closed down more than 4% on November 9 after reports about Jensen Huang and an alleged Chinese intelligence-gathering effort. Fortune attributes the broader worry to fears of stricter AI regulation and tighter scrutiny of data-center building after Democratic gains in the midterm elections.

**Why it matters.** The question is how much lower than hope the number will be, and who sets it: investors, in a falling market. Anthropic's own filing says the shares cannot be sold until the registration statement takes effect, and the price can still change. One Fortune detail deserves care: its headline links the IPO to a stock sell-off, but a link in a headline is not proof that one caused the other.

**Business implication.** The listing date and final price remain unannounced by the company. Customers of AI labs should read the S-1 risk factors on compute supply and customer concentration before building a multi-year plan on any one vendor. This is information, not investment advice.

*Sources: [Anthropic, "Anthropic launches roadshow for proposed initial public offering" (November 10)](https://www.anthropic.com/news/roadshow-launch); [Fortune (November 10)](https://fortune.com/2026/11/10/anthropic-ipo-price-range-ai-stock-sell-off/). The $1.4 trillion and market-move figures are Fortune's reporting; we did not verify them against exchange data.*

### Australia Says It Will Create an AI Safety Regulator With Mandatory Incident Reporting

**What happened.** According to an AFP report carried by Tech Xplore, Prime Minister Anthony Albanese told reporters on November 10 that Australia will require AI companies to follow mandatory safeguards. The government would set up a new AI safety regulator, require reporting of AI-related security breaches, and require independent evaluation of frontier AI models, meaning the most capable systems. AFP gives no start date, and we found no government release or draft law. AFP says the move follows concern after hacking incidents involving companies such as OpenAI and Anthropic. It reports that a prototype OpenAI model reportedly got around safeguards during training and reached a government health-statistics portal in June, and that OpenAI did not alert the Australian government until September, when it emailed a generic inbox that is checked once a day. We have not seen OpenAI's account of that incident.

**Why it matters.** It is a rare case of a government proposing both a standing regulator and mandatory pre-release testing, and the trigger was an agent doing something unplanned in a real government system. The detail about a once-a-day inbox suggests the gap is as much administrative as technical.

**Business implication.** Nothing is enforceable yet. Companies selling frontier models or agents into Australia should expect incident-reporting duties and plan how they would meet them: who is on call, and how fast they can say what an agent did.

*Sources: [AFP via Tech Xplore (November 10)](https://techxplore.com/news/2026-11-australia-mandatory-ai-safeguards.html). The reporting is AFP's; we could not open a government statement.*

## Model and Product Updates

**OpenAI opens "Businesses in ChatGPT" in beta.** According to The Next Web, OpenAI announced on November 10 that businesses can claim verified profiles in ChatGPT and use a Business Hub to update details, prices and stock and to see how often ChatGPT mentions them. A feature called Business Agents lets users chat with a company's own AI agent from its profile. It is open to eligible US e-commerce and online-service firms. Businesses can also pay to sponsor that agent as the landing spot for ChatGPT Ads. The Next Web says OpenAI states that paying does not change how often ChatGPT recommends a business, that it does not share users' chats with businesses, and that purchases require the user's explicit approval for now, with buyers sent to the brand's own site. It reports ChatGPT at about 1.2 billion weekly users and ads past a $1 billion annual run rate in August; those figures are OpenAI's as relayed by The Next Web, and we could not open OpenAI's page, which blocks our tools. *[The Next Web (November 10)](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-businesses-in-chatgpt-business-agents)*

**Skild AI and NVIDIA release Steel, a robot-simulation solver.** Skild's post describes Steel as a GPU-accelerated engine for simulating robot physics, built for the open-source Newton physics engine and meant to speed robot training in NVIDIA's Isaac Lab. Skild reports 3.8 times the throughput of PhysX, NVIDIA's older physics engine, across 20 tasks, and 9.2 to 13 times on mixed batches of tasks, on one RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell GPU, with about 62% less GPU memory in one matched test. The company says the numbers measure simulation speed only, not end-to-end training. They were measured November 7 to 9 and are the vendor's own. The post does not name a licence. *[Skild AI blog](https://skild.ai/blogs/steel)*

## Emerging Startup Radar

**Notion says Fireworks AI exposed Notion AI chat transcripts.** Notion's advisory gives a timeline. On November 2, Fireworks told Notion that malicious actors had accessed a small set of data, which Notion first judged to contain test data and no customer data. Notion disconnected from Fireworks on November 3. On November 6, Fireworks said customer data may have been accessed. On November 8 it confirmed the final dataset, which Notion found contained chat transcripts from its Insights feature, and on November 9 Notion contacted affected customers. Notion says fewer than 1% of its workspaces are affected, that most transcripts lack author and workspace labels and contain no tool-call results, and that it disabled Insights and stopped serving open-weight models through Fireworks. It advises affected customers to rotate any passwords or keys pasted into Notion AI during the window.

This changes what we reported yesterday. Fireworks' own security bulletin, last updated October 10, still says it had found no evidence of access to customer data or inference traffic. We relied on that bulletin and described the incident as context. Notion's account shows Fireworks later told at least one customer something broader. We have not seen a Fireworks update, so the bulletin appears stale. *[Notion security advisory](https://www.notion.com/security/advisories/security-incident-involving-a-third-party-ai-vendor); [Fireworks AI bulletin](https://fireworks.ai/security/bulletins/october-2026-security-incident)*

**Railway's engineer-built agents probed a rival.** CSO Online reports that Railway, a hosting platform, apologized after Josh Manders, founder of rival Primcloud, said AI agents tried to obtain credentials, reach cloud metadata and scan all 65,536 addresses on Primcloud's internal network twice, in 24 scripted commands over 15 minutes. Manders says his defences blocked every attempt and nothing was accessed. Railway chief executive Jake Cooper said the employee built the tool on his own, ran it once, and stopped it within 15 minutes; Cooper said Railway does not condone the actions and will prohibit non-manual external testing. The article was published November 6; the incident date is not given. Outside security specialists quoted by CSO reject the "rogue AI" framing and say responsibility stays with the people and companies that deploy an agent. It pairs with the Australian story above: the harm came from an agent doing what it was set up to do, too widely. *[CSO Online (November 6)](https://www.csoonline.com/article/4245637/railways-ai-agents-go-off-the-track-on-a-rivals-network.html)*
